Lesson 03: Current and its measurement

Year 10 Science 1 · 23 Oct 2026 · Completed

Inquiry question Does current change as it travels around a series circuit?

Classes

Y10 Science 1

Date

23 October 2026

Suggested duration

50–60 minutes

Lesson

4 of 12

Unit

Electricity and circuits

Teacher

Haaris

Whakataukī

Mā pango, mā whero, ka oti ai te mahi.

Meaning: Through different contributions, the work will be completed.

Why this lesson: Supports shared practical roles when connecting meters and recording reliable current measurements.

Curriculum alignment

Knowledge

  • Electric current is the rate at which electric charge moves through a conductor, measured in amperes (A).
  • Electric circuits provide a continuous path that allows charge to move through components.

Practices

  • Construct and test series and parallel circuits to observe current flow and circuit function.
  • Measure current and voltage in different parts of a circuit using appropriate instruments.
  • Use appropriate scientific conventions when reporting about electricity.

Learning intention

We are learning to measure current and interpret what current tells us about moving charge.

Success criteria

  • I can connect an ammeter in series.
  • I can record current with a value and unit.
  • I can use measurements to evaluate whether current is used up.

Resources

  • Cells, lamps or resistors, leads and switches
  • Ammeters or multimeters
  • Circuit diagrams
  • Results table

Safety

Use low-voltage supplies. Check meter settings before connection. Never connect an ammeter directly across a cell.

Teaching and learning sequence

Time and phase

Teacher actions

Student actions

Assessment and responsive support

8 min

Elicit ideas

Display positions 1, 2 and 3, with position 3 between cell and lamp; elicit comparisons and reasons.

Predict the relative readings individually and explain reasoning.

Record differing ideas for comparison after measurement.

10 min

Meter modelling

Recall current and amperes; model series ammeter connection, polarity and range selection.

Identify the meter position, setting and safe connection procedure.

Check meter understanding before releasing groups to work.

25 min

Measurement

Assign builder, checker, meter reader and recorder. Check circuits and supervise moving the ammeter with the circuit disconnected. Prompt comparison of positions while keeping components unchanged.

Build and check the loop, read steady current in A and record position/readings. Everyone predicts, discusses and draws the circuit in their own book.

Check safe meter use, participation, diagrams and paired position/readings. Ask groups to identify the pattern before explaining it.

12 min

Reasoning / Plenary

Reasoning and plenary (12 min): use 7 minutes for evidence-based comparison, doubtful readings and consolidation after students explain results. Reserve 5 minutes for the existing individual exit response and a summary linked to the learning intention.

Explain whether measurements support the prediction; recheck doubtful values where feasible. Complete the individual evidence-based exit response.

Check whether students distinguish energy transfer in the lamp from charge consumption. Use responses to plan the next lesson; retain existing fault-finding extension arrangements where applicable.

Assessment for learning

Check initial predictions, meter setup, recorded values and units, and whether students revise their thinking using evidence. Use the individual exit response to assess their final claim.

Support and extension

Provide meter diagrams and fixed ranges. Extend students by discussing measurement uncertainty and small differences between readings.

Questions to guide discussion

  • Why must the ammeter become part of the path?
  • What pattern appears in the readings?
  • After the investigation: if current is not used up, what changes in the lamp?

Post lesson evaluation

Y10 Science 1: 23 October 2026

The students were engaged, and they were able to work through the circuit experiment independently. The discussions also went well. The experiment finished about ten minutes earlier than I had allowed for, which left a gap in the planned sequence. I used that time for students to complete unfinished worksheets from earlier lessons and for conversations that helped me get to know them better.

Although this was a useful way to use the remaining time, I would prefer to have an extension ready that develops the learning from the investigation. Finishing the practical quickly does not by itself show that everyone understands why the readings are similar at different positions. In the next delivery, I will use the students' measurements and explanations to check this, retain a brief individual exit response, and have a circuit fault-finding task available if the group finishes early. I will keep the timing flexible because the other group may need a different amount of support.

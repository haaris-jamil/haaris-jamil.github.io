Professional Experience 1

Lesson 07: Independently switched parallel lamps and electrical protection

Year 10 Science 1 · 6 Nov 2026 · Completed

Full lesson plan | Year 10 Science | Teacher: Haaris Jamil | Duration: 60 minutes

Class: Y10 Science 1 Planned date: 6 November 2026 Period / room: as confirmed timetable

Lesson purpose and prior learning

This lesson builds on the connection and inspection skills developed in Science 1 Lesson 7 on 4 November. The practical and testing took longer and the exit check was not completed. Use a supplied one-lamp, one-switch circuit diagram as the starting point. Students will modify it to add a second lamp and switch with independent control. All groups completed the outlet task, and one group began the next task; check that group's starting point and retain an extension. Students will construct two independently switched parallel lamp branches, test all four switch combinations, and explain independent operation. The closing heating demonstration and electrical-protection discussion follow only after student practical work and equipment pack-up.

Whakataukī

He waka eke noa.

Meaning: We are all in this together.

Justification: Students share equipment and responsibility, supporting one another to build, check and test independently switched parallel lamps.

Curriculum alignment

Knowledge

  • Electric circuits provide a continuous path that allows charge to move through components.
  • Conductors (e.g. metals) allow the movement of electric charge.
  • Insulators (e.g. plastic) restrict the movement of electric charge.
  • Parallel circuits have multiple paths for current, where voltage is the same across branches and branch currents combine to form total current. A break in one path does not stop current in others.

Practices

  • Construct and test series and parallel circuits to observe current flow and circuit function.
  • Use appropriate scientific conventions when reporting about electricity.

Learning intention

We are learning to construct and explain two independently switched parallel lamp circuits, then relate electrical heating to household protective devices.

Success criteria

  • I can construct two parallel branches, each containing a switch and a 12 V AC lamp.
  • I can test all four switch combinations and record both lamps’ states accurately.
  • I can explain independent operation using the complete path in each branch.
  • I can recognise that excessive current and residual-current imbalance require different protective functions.

Resources

Separate changeover switches, two 12 V AC-rated lamps and suitable holders per practical set, compatible leads/cable, tools, continuity meters, protected connectors and an isolated 12 V AC school supply. The Lesson 8 worksheet includes the parallel diagram and results table. Outlet fixtures are not part of this lesson.

The functional test supply requires verified safety isolation and current limiting or fusing matched to the cable, fittings and combined lamp load. The teacher-only closing demonstration requires purpose-built enclosed heating-effect apparatus, a visible temperature indication, manufacturer instructions and the school’s approved operating procedure. Pictures or disconnected examples of an MCB and RCCB will support the closing explanation.

Before the lesson I will test the two-branch circuit, verify the supply protection and identify common and the selected contact on each switch. I will prepare the same fittings as those used by students. I will check the enclosed heating apparatus within its specified operating range and arrange the demonstration area separately from the student equipment.

The heating demonstration is conditional on suitable enclosed apparatus and its approved operating procedure. Where these are unavailable, I will use an illustration for the closing explanation. The student activity remains the construction and testing of the working parallel circuit shown on the worksheet.

Safety and organisation

Students will prepare, connect, inspect and modify their assemblies with the supply disconnected. I will inspect each assembly before controlling its connection to the 12 V AC supply. Conducting parts will be enclosed or otherwise protected during powered testing. Each assembly will be disconnected before it is returned to students for handling or adjustment.

I will use verified supply isolation, appropriately rated output protection and secured fittings throughout the practical work. The student circuits will be inspected before powered testing and disconnected before corrections. After all student equipment has been collected, I will operate the enclosed heating apparatus within its normal rated limits, retain a clear observation boundary, allow cooling and stop for any unexpected odour, heating or equipment behaviour. The demonstration will show a controlled temperature rise rather than wire failure or a deliberately created short circuit.

Assemblies will be labelled “12 V AC teaching model only”, kept separate from equipment in mains service and retained or dismantled at school. Student assemblies will have no mains supply plug. Powered testing will take place once the supply, protected connections and compatible load have been checked; disconnected inspection and continuity testing provide the alternative where these requirements cannot be met.

Student construction will follow the working two-branch diagram. I will collect all apparatus before introducing fault conditions in the closing explanation. The isolated 12 V AC model represents circuit behaviour; household MCB and RCCB operation will be explained through diagrams or disconnected examples.

Teaching and learning sequence

Teacher activity and timing

Student activity

0–5 min | Retrieval

I will provide a diagram containing a supply, one lamp and one switch. Students will modify it by adding a second lamp and a second switch so that each switch controls only its own lamp. I will use their proposed paths to identify misconceptions and decide who needs support before showing the completed construction diagram. Where needed, I will prompt students to trace the existing path first.

Students will work individually on the supplied diagram in worksheet Section 1, adding a second lamp and switch for independent control. They will briefly explain their proposed paths. Those who finish early will trace the complete path when either switch is open.

5–10 min | Introduce the parallel task

I will introduce the whakataukī and model tracing the two branches in the diagram. I will identify the selected contacts on the separate switches and demonstrate a connection check. The task will use switches and lamps directly.

Students will identify the two branches and locate one switch and lamp in each. Partners will agree practical roles and identify where they need support.

10–32 min | Paired construction

I will circulate and check first connections. An annotated diagram and stepwise prompts will support learners who need them; ready pairs will construct from the basic diagram and trace the completed paths independently.

Students will construct two parallel branches, each containing one switch and one 12 V AC lamp. Both partners will make and check connections. They will present the disconnected circuit for inspection.

32–44 min | Functional tests and troubleshooting

I will inspect circuits and control the protected 12 V AC tests. I will allow students to record each of the four switch combinations before discussing explanations. Corrections will take place after disconnection.

Students will record both lamps’ states in all four switch combinations. If a result differs from the intended operation, they will trace the diagram and explain a proposed correction before requesting another check.

44–50 min | Practical review and equipment pack-up

I will ask learners to use their results to explain independent switching and complete Section 3. I will disconnect and collect all student circuits, leads and tools before the closing demonstration. Students will retain their worksheets for the safety discussion.

Students will complete their practical explanations individually, then pack away all apparatus and move to the observation area with their worksheets. Ready learners will explain what happens when one branch is disconnected.

50–60 min | Closing demonstration, followed by MCBs and RCCBs

After the two-lamp experiment, practical explanations and equipment pack-up are complete, I will give the teacher-only controlled heating demonstration using purpose-built enclosed apparatus within its rated limits. I will relate this to excessive current in a short circuit, then introduce MCBs as overcurrent protection and RCCBs as residual-current protection. During the discussion, I will pause for students to complete the brief notes in Section 4. I will check their responses orally and collect the worksheets at the end.

Students will observe from the designated area, participate in the safety discussion and complete the brief prompts in Section 4. They will submit their worksheets at the end of the lesson.

Assessment and differentiation

I will assess construction and checking skills, each learner’s participation, the four-state results table and individual explanations of the conducting paths. Worksheets will be collected after the closing safety discussion. The brief notes and oral responses will provide an initial check of understanding of short circuits, MCBs and RCCBs, which will be revisited in the next lesson’s retrieval activity.

Students needing support will use the annotated branch diagram, a staged connection checklist and a first-connection check. I will help them trace one branch before moving to the second. Ready learners will construct from the basic diagram and explain how a disconnected branch affects the circuit. Both partners will alternate practical roles, and individual responses will provide a check beyond the pair’s completed circuit.

Students who finish early will annotate the complete paths for the four switch combinations or diagnose a disconnected fault selected by me. These extensions will be completed during the student practical phase and concluded before pack-up.

Inspection checklist

  • Connections match the checked diagram and actual terminal markings.
  • Conductors are securely retained using the component manufacturer’s method.
  • Insulation remains intact; exposed conductor is appropriately contained and stray strands are controlled.
  • Cable support, enclosure and covers are suitable for the test arrangement.
  • The assembly is disconnected and ready for inspection and continuity testing.

Scientific understanding

A short circuit is an unintended low-resistance path that can allow excessive current. Loose or high-resistance contacts can also cause local heating without a short circuit. The closing demonstration will illustrate the heating effect of current within the apparatus’s rated range. It will not reproduce an actual fault or establish the cause of previous classroom circuit failures. Lesson 8 worksheet: View portfolio evidence

Parallel branches provide separate conducting paths. Opening the switch in one branch interrupts that branch while the other can remain complete. Students will use the results table to explain the four observed states before the teacher’s closing demonstration.

An MCB provides overcurrent protection against overloads and short circuits. An RCCB is a residual-current device (RCD) that detects an imbalance between outgoing and returning current; it does not provide overload or short-circuit protection. An RCBO combines overcurrent and residual-current protection. The controlled heating demonstration introduces why overcurrent protection matters; residual-current protection is a separate function explained using a diagram or disconnected device.

Practical review before the closing demonstration

  • Use the results table to explain what happens to Lamp 2 when only Switch 1 changes.
  • Name one connection-quality check and the problem it helps identify.
  • Explain why one complete parallel branch can operate when the other is open.

Timing and carryover

Student construction, testing, practical explanations and equipment pack-up will finish before the closing demonstration at 50 minutes. The final 10 minutes include the demonstration, MCB and RCCB discussion, brief worksheet notes and an oral check. I will collect worksheets at the end. If practical work takes longer, I will preserve the review and safe pack-up, and carry the demonstration and safety discussion into the next lesson.

Evidence of learning

Evidence will include anonymised circuit photographs, individual participation notes, the four-state results table, corrections and individual explanations. I will record the actual stopping point and whether the closing demonstration took place. The closing safety notes and oral responses will provide initial evidence of understanding of short circuits, MCBs and RCCBs; later retrieval will check retention.

Post-lesson reflection

Y10 Science 1 | Delivered: 6 November 2026

The lesson went really well. The students were highly engaged, and most groups completed the two-lamp challenge. As in the previous practical lesson, they responded enthusiastically to a task with a clear connection to the world outside the classroom.

We did not complete the full planned sequence, but this was not unexpected. I had deliberately planned more activities than we might complete in an hour so that there would be meaningful work available if students finished early. I therefore do not see the unfinished sequence, on its own, as a problem. The time was spent on an activity that engaged the learners and gave them an opportunity to build, test and troubleshoot their circuits. The remaining activities will need to be revisited in the next lesson.

My biggest takeaway from the last couple of lessons is the importance of knowing the students well and planning accordingly. I might not have chosen this particular activity for another class, but it worked very well for this group. My mentor teacher’s knowledge of their interests and what engages them has been particularly valuable in shaping these lessons. Effective planning is not only about the content or the activity itself; it is also about understanding the learners who will be taking part. I want to carry this forward by using what I learn about students to select contexts and activities that make the scientific ideas meaningful to them.

Lesson 10: Local beach electricity-generation investigation

Year 10 Science 1 · 18 Nov 2026 · Planned

Inquiry question How can moving air or falling water generate electricity in a small model?

Classes

Y10 Science 1

Date

18 November 2026 - provisional

Suggested duration

60 minutes of learning plus walking time – agree a suitable visit window with my mentor teacher

Lesson

11 of 12

Unit

Electricity and circuits

Teacher

Haaris

Whakataukī

Toitū te marae a Tāne, toitū te marae a Tangaroa, toitū te iwi.

Meaning: If land and sea are sustained, the people will prosper.

Why this lesson: Connects the beach investigation with caring for land and sea while exploring electricity generation.

Curriculum alignment

Knowledge

  • Multiple methods are used to generate electricity (e.g. turbines, solar panels). Note: In-depth understanding of electromagnetic induction is not expected at this level.
  • Energy is transferred between components in a circuit as electric charge moves and interacts with those components.

Practices

  • Measure current and voltage in different parts of a circuit using appropriate instruments.
  • Research methods and implications of electricity generation (e.g. turbines, solar panels) and evaluate their relevance to New Zealand.

Learning intention

We are learning to compare turbine setups using the number of LEDs lit on a shared circuit board and explain how movement can generate electricity.

Success criteria

  • I can describe the energy-transfer chain from wind or stored water to the turbine/generator and electrical output.
  • I can record the number of LEDs lit in each trial, including zero, and describe the test conditions.
  • I can compare two setups and identify one limitation of using LED count as an indicator of output.

Resources

  • Student-built turbine prototypes, a bench-tested generator connection and one shared multi-LED circuit board. Confirm the board’s LED total and connection with my mentor teacher before the visit. No voltage readings are taken.
  • Recording sheet: model, feature changed, conditions, LED counts for each trial, conclusion and limitation.
  • Wind option: securely mounted rotors in the approved test area. Water option, if approved: teacher-controlled, contained flow using carried water and a collection tray. Keep the generator, board and connections dry.
  • Clipboards, pencils, school-approved recording equipment if needed; agreed walking route and visit arrangements

Safety

Obtain school EOTC approval and follow the site-specific risk assessment, supervision and consent arrangements. Confirm the beach/site and route with my mentor teacher; check weather, tide, boundaries and accessibility. Work in the approved area away from surf and water entry. Keep hands clear of moving rotors. I will check the setup and control connection of the dry LED board. Use a contained water model; do not dam natural waterways or alter habitat. Take materials away after use. Provide an equivalent school-based fan/water-tray investigation if access, weather or learner needs prevent participation.

Teaching and learning sequence

Time and phase

Teacher actions

Student actions

Assessment and responsive support

8 min

Preparation at school

Confirm the bench-tested wind or contained-water option with my mentor teacher and explain the investigation conditions and energy-transfer focus.

Predict the energy-transfer chain and identify what conditions to record.

Check readiness, safe method and the limitations of varying wind or water flow.

8 min

Model setup

Use the separately agreed walking allocation; establish site boundaries and roles, check rotor security and confirm the board and connections are dry, and oversee setup. Site, date, route and option remain subject to agreement.

Follow the agreed route, boundaries and roles; help set up the approved model.

Check access, supervision and equipment readiness before investigation.

22 min

Investigate

Connect the shared LED board after checking each setup. Supervise repeated trials. Ask groups to change one feature at a time and keep the board and other conditions as similar as practicable. Record wind changes or, for a contained-water test, any change in flow.

Count and record how many LEDs light in each trial, including zero. Repeat the test and compare the two setups.

Check comparability and limitations; natural wind is variable. Keep the generator, board and connections dry and follow the existing EOTC arrangements.

15 min

Explain and compare

Guide the energy-transfer explanation and comparison with another New Zealand generation method; connect to Purkal's electricity access.

Draw the chain, use the LED counts to explain findings and identify model limits.

Check that students distinguish the LED count from a measurement of voltage or power. Zero lit LEDs does not necessarily mean zero electrical output.

7 min

Reflect and collect / Plenary

Plenary and pack-up (7 min): reserve about 4 minutes for a claim, supporting LED count, limitation and improvement; summarise learning, then direct equipment collection and site care. Walking and extended cleanup are allocated separately.

Write the brief evidence-based conclusion, submit it, then pack away and leave the site as found.

Review energy-transfer chains, measurement conditions and limitations. Use findings to decide what needs consolidating back at school.

Assessment for learning

Collect a prediction, a setup sketch, repeated LED counts with test conditions, an energy-transfer chain and a conclusion with one limitation.

Support and extension

Provide a labelled model, clear roles, vocabulary and a partly completed results table. If the trip cannot go ahead, use the school-approved fan or contained-water setup and the same LED board. Extend by asking students how they could improve the fairness of the comparison.

Questions to guide discussion

  • What changes from wind or a raised water reservoir through the turbine and generator?
  • How many LEDs lit, and what else might have affected the count?
  • What can this model tell us about real generation, and what can it not establish?

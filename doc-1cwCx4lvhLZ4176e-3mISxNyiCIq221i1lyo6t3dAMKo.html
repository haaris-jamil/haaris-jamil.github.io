Year 13 Physics Demonstration Plan – Standing Waves on a String

DEMONSTRATION OVERVIEW

Class: Year 13 Physics

Delivered:

Role: Planned and led

Suggested duration: 15–25 minutes

Inquiry question: How can two travelling waves combine to produce a stationary pattern?

LEARNING INTENTION

Students explain the formation of standing waves through superposition and reflection, identify nodes and antinodes, and connect resonant patterns with driving frequency.

SUCCESS CRITERIA

Students identify nodes and antinodes; explain the pattern as superposition of incident and reflected waves; relate changing frequency to the observed modes; and use L = nλ/2 for a string with nodes at both ends where appropriate.

EQUIPMENT

Signal generator, vibration generator, light string, clamp stands, secure attachment points, metre rule or tape measure, and a suitable tensioning arrangement such as a pulley and securely contained mass if required by the apparatus.

PREPARATION AND SETUP

Connect the signal generator to the vibration generator according to the equipment instructions. Secure the string and establish steady tension. Begin at low amplitude. Check that the stands, clamps, pulley and any hanging mass are stable and that the moving string has a clear exclusion zone.

DEMONSTRATION SEQUENCE

Time and phase

Teacher actions

Student actions

Assessment and responsive support

3 min

Predict

Ask what may change as driving frequency increases, including node spacing and number.

Predict and give a reason.

Record ideas to revisit after observing modes.

3 min

Travelling disturbance

Operate at low amplitude; identify the incident wave and explain reflection at the boundary.

Observe and identify the incident and reflected waves.

Check prior understanding of reflection and superposition.

5 min

Find resonance

Increase frequency gradually within rated settings until a stable pattern appears; ask about minimum and maximum displacement.

Locate and label nodes and antinodes.

Check the distinction between a node and an antinode.

5 min

Compare modes

Locate at least two resonances and prompt comparisons.

Count loops, estimate wavelength from node spacing and record frequency.

Check that comparisons use the same string length and tension.

5 min

Explanation and plenary

Use a diagram to consolidate constructive/destructive interference and boundary conditions; apply L = nλ/2 and v = fλ where appropriate. Reserve the final 2 minutes for an individual sketch and brief superposition explanation.

Label nodes and antinodes and explain how incident and reflected waves create the stationary pattern; compare with the initial prediction.

Review sketches and explanations to identify confusion about stationary patterns. These explicit checks are refinements for a future delivery; retain the original delivery evaluation below.

GUIDING QUESTIONS

Why do some driving frequencies produce a clearer pattern? What happens at a node? How does the number of antinodes change as resonant frequency increases? How would changing tension or string length affect the resonant frequencies?

ASSESSMENT AND EVIDENCE

Students label nodes and antinodes on a sketch, explain the pattern using superposition and predict the effect of increasing frequency. Evidence may include the annotated sketch, verbal responses, recorded frequencies and a photograph or short video where permitted.

SAFETY

Use both generators within their rated settings. Begin at low amplitude, keep hands and faces clear of the moving string, secure stands and hanging masses, keep leads out of walkways, and switch off before adjusting the string or tension.

POST-DEMONSTRATION EVALUATION

The demonstration was delivered with a brief explanation of the underlying principles. Next time, ask students to predict the effect of each frequency change before the trial and use follow-up questions or a labelled sketch to check individual understanding.

PLANNING NOTE

The school purchased the signal generator following the teacher’s recommendation, enabling the demonstration to be conducted with suitable classroom equipment.

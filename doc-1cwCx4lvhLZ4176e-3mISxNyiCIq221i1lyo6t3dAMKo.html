Knowledge statement(s)

Practices statement(s)

Standing waves form through the superposition of incident and reflected waves. Nodes have zero displacement, while antinodes have maximum displacement. Resonant frequencies depend on the string’s length, tension and wave speed.

Use observations and wave models to explain resonant patterns and relate measurements to wavelength and frequency.

Capability

Focus in this lesson

Communication

The students will describe what stays still and what moves, then use nodes, antinodes and superposition to explain the pattern. A labelled sketch gives them another way to make their understanding clear.

Learning Focus

Key science ideas

The demonstration shows how two travelling waves can produce a stationary pattern. The students will identify nodes and antinodes, compare resonant modes and relate node spacing to wavelength. For a string with nodes at both ends, L = nλ/2 can describe the permitted patterns.

Prior knowledge

The students need an understanding of travelling waves, reflection, superposition and v = fλ. I will check these ideas before increasing the driving frequency.

Science skills and Nature of Science

The students will predict, observe and compare modes, use a labelled sketch to represent the pattern and explain how the model accounts for it.

Learning Aims

Learning Intentions

Success Criteria

Explain standing-wave formation through superposition and reflection, identify nodes and antinodes, and relate resonant patterns to driving frequency.

Identify nodes and antinodes in an observed pattern.

Explain how incident and reflected waves produce the stationary pattern.

Relate changing frequency to the observed modes.

Use L = nλ/2 for a string with nodes at both ends where appropriate.

Teaching/Pedagogical Approaches

I will use a constructed model to make the stationary pattern visible. Predictions will give the students a reason to compare successive modes, while the diagram will connect what they see with the superposition explanation. I will establish the observation first, then use it to consolidate the wave model.

Assessment Opportunities/Methods

I will ask for a prediction before changing the frequency, check the distinction between nodes and antinodes, and ask how reflection contributes to the pattern. A final labelled sketch and brief explanation will provide an individual check.

Duration: 21 minutes

0–3 min

Predict

I will ask what might change as the driving frequency increases, including the number and spacing of nodes. I will record ideas to revisit after the demonstration.

The students will make a prediction and give a reason.

3–6 min

Travelling waves

I will operate the apparatus at low amplitude, identify the incident wave and explain reflection at the boundary. I will check the students’ prior understanding of superposition.

The students will observe and identify the incident and reflected waves.

6–11 min

Find resonance

I will gradually change the frequency within the rated settings until a stable pattern appears. I will ask where displacement is smallest and greatest.

The students will identify and label nodes and antinodes, distinguishing the stationary pattern from the string’s motion.

11–16 min

Compare modes

I will locate at least two resonances while keeping string length and tension unchanged, and ask the students to compare them.

The students will count loops, estimate wavelength from node spacing and record the frequency.

16–21 min

Explain and close

I will use a diagram to consolidate interference and boundary conditions, applying L = nλ/2 and v = fλ where appropriate. The final two minutes will be used for an individual sketch and explanation.

The students will label nodes and antinodes, explain the superposition of incident and reflected waves and compare the result with their prediction.

General Information

Materials, Resources or Equipment Required

Signal generator, vibration generator, light string, clamp stands, secure attachment points, metre rule or tape measure, and a suitable tensioning arrangement. I will connect the equipment according to its instructions, secure the string and establish steady tension before class.

Safety Considerations

I will use both generators within their rated settings, beginning at low amplitude. Stands, clamps, pulleys and any hanging mass will be secured. Students will keep hands and faces clear of the moving string, and leads will remain clear of walkways. I will switch off before adjusting the string or tension.

Year 13 Physics Demonstration Plan – Standing Waves on a String

DEMONSTRATION OVERVIEW

Class: Year 13 Physics

Delivered: Sep 23, 2026

Role: Planned and led

Suggested duration: 15–25 minutes

Inquiry question: How can two travelling waves combine to produce a stationary pattern?

LEARNING INTENTION

Students explain the formation of standing waves through superposition and reflection, identify nodes and antinodes, and connect resonant patterns with driving frequency.

SUCCESS CRITERIA

Students identify nodes and antinodes; explain the pattern as superposition of incident and reflected waves; relate changing frequency to the observed modes; and use L = nλ/2 for a string with nodes at both ends where appropriate.

EQUIPMENT

Signal generator, vibration generator, light string, clamp stands, secure attachment points, metre rule or tape measure, and a suitable tensioning arrangement such as a pulley and securely contained mass if required by the apparatus.

PREPARATION AND SETUP

Connect the signal generator to the vibration generator according to the equipment instructions. Secure the string and establish steady tension. Begin at low amplitude. Check that the stands, clamps, pulley and any hanging mass are stable and that the moving string has a clear exclusion zone.

Explanation and plenary

Use a diagram to consolidate constructive/destructive interference and boundary conditions; apply L = nλ/2 and v = fλ where appropriate. Reserve the final 2 minutes for an individual sketch and brief superposition explanation.

Label nodes and antinodes and explain how incident and reflected waves create the stationary pattern; compare with the initial prediction.

Review sketches and explanations to identify confusion about stationary patterns. These explicit checks are refinements for a future delivery; retain the original delivery evaluation below.

GUIDING QUESTIONS

Why do some driving frequencies produce a clearer pattern? What happens at a node? How does the number of antinodes change as resonant frequency increases? How would changing tension or string length affect the resonant frequencies?

ASSESSMENT AND EVIDENCE

Students label nodes and antinodes on a sketch, explain the pattern using superposition and predict the effect of increasing frequency. Evidence may include the annotated sketch, verbal responses, recorded frequencies and a photograph or short video where permitted.

SAFETY

Use both generators within their rated settings. Begin at low amplitude, keep hands and faces clear of the moving string, secure stands and hanging masses, keep leads out of walkways, and switch off before adjusting the string or tension.

POST-DEMONSTRATION EVALUATION

The demonstration was delivered with a brief explanation of the underlying principles. Next time, ask students to predict the effect of each frequency change before the trial and use follow-up questions or a labelled sketch to check individual understanding.

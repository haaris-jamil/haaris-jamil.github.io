Week 4 Reflection

19–23 October 2026

This reflection uses the “What? So what? Now what?” framework of Rolfe et al. (2001).

What?

This week I continued teaching the Year 10 electricity unit. In the lesson that included my pepeha, students asked many questions and requested that I deliver part of the lesson in Hindi, which I did. The assessment took about five minutes longer than planned, leaving the circuit activity rushed. We completed the lesson, but I used oral responses for the exit ticket instead of written answers.

In another lesson, students worked through the ammeter investigation independently and the discussions went well. They finished about ten minutes early. I used this time to complete an earlier worksheet and get to know them better. my mentor teacher’s informal feedback was encouraging: he felt students were engaged and learning. He suggested occasionally following “Any questions?” with a question of my own to check understanding.

I also observed the EOTC teacher managing the Year 7–8 beach investigation through measurement tasks, physical activity and calmly waiting for everyone’s attention. At Sea Dogs, my mentor teacher and the more experienced students supported beginners, including me.

So what?

The contrasting lesson timings showed me that a coherent plan still needs room for questions and differences in how quickly students work. Finishing an activity, or appearing engaged, does not by itself show that every student has understood. My oral exit questions gave me some useful information, but less evidence of individual understanding than a short written response would have. my mentor teacher’s suggestion reminded me that having no questions is not the same as understanding.

Sharing my identity and using the spare time for conversations created opportunities for relationship building. The outdoor experiences reinforced this: support from teachers and peers helped me participate as a beginner. the EOTC teacher’s patient approach also gave me a practical example of maintaining expectations without rushing or speaking over students.

I am making progress towards translating the curriculum into a unit and responsive lesson plans, constructing visual models, and learning to organise a local field trip. My experience of participating in trips has grown, but I still need to understand the safety procedures and documentation before taking a fuller planning role.

Now what?

For the next delivery, I have prepared a separate fault-finding challenge using circuit diagrams and concepts students already know. I will allow more time for questions while protecting the practical activity and a brief individual exit response. I will also prepare targeted questions after explanations and use students’ answers and marked work to decide what needs revisiting.

I will continue developing physical models with my mentor teacher and check their usefulness through students’ explanations and application of the concepts. I will discuss field-trip safety protocols, risk assessment and documentation with him so I can contribute more fully to planning a curriculum-linked local trip.

References

Rolfe, G., Freshwater, D., & Jasper, M. (2001). Critical reflection for nursing and the helping professions: A user’s guide. Palgrave.

Rough notes

Week 4 – Rough notes

19–23 October 2026

Monday – 19 October

Year 10 Science 2: change of plan

I had been away on Friday and could not set up the circuits intended for Lesson 3. my mentor teacher and I discussed an alternative about half an hour before the lesson and photocopied a short assessment based on the previous lesson.

We took the class to a small seating area at the edge of the school, where students completed the assessment. We then walked through the estuary area and returned to school.

My mentor teacher led almost the entire lesson. Planning was discussed together, but delivery was mainly his. I have recorded this as an observation of adapting a lesson when the intended practical activity was not ready. It is my seventh observation of others.

The circuit activity was not delivered. I still need to look at the assessment responses before claiming what the students understood.

Lunchtime walk with my mentor teacher

My mentor teacher and I walked through the school during lunch while he was on duty. I got to see the primary children playing around, seemingly without a care in the world.

I asked him about the gender-based self-segregation I had noticed in seating and break-time groups. He said he had not noticed it. When I gave him examples, he agreed that it was happening, but could not say why.

There are exceptions to the pattern. At this point, I do not know what explains it or whether it affects participation or belonging. my mentor teacher agreeing with my examples does not explain the cause.

Year 13 exam preparation

Worked with a Year 13 student on exam preparation today. For planning, I looked at the syllabus, past papers and mark schemes.

I also reported a session on Thursday, 15 October, with the same planning approach. Both have been added as backup learning events, with eligibility towards the required total still unconfirmed.

Specific question topics, student responses and evidence of progress have not yet been recorded. The earlier 14 October session remains a separate entry.

Preparing the next Lesson 3 deliveries

My mentor teacher took Lesson 2 with Y10 Science 1 during my absence. My next lesson will be my first interaction with that class since then, so I need to start with my pepeha and allow fifteen minutes.

The remaining forty-five minutes need to focus on building, controlling and explaining a complete circuit. Keep the recap short, preserve hands-on time, and finish with a brief independent check.

Y10 Science 2 needs a separate deck without the pepeha. Start with completion of the Lesson 2 worksheet as a Do now recap, then use the extra fifteen minutes for a meaningful predict–test–explain materials investigation.

These are preparation decisions, not lessons already delivered.

Observation visit arrangements

Prepared a timetable showing Year 10 lessons within the full school day. Senior lessons are blank; Duty and SeaDogs have been removed.

Drafted a reply to my visiting lecturer indicating that any Year 10 class and any week between 26 October and 13 November is suitable. Sending the reply has not been confirmed in these notes.

Follow-up

Test and set out the circuit kits before the next lesson.

Review the short assessment and completed Lesson 2 worksheets; use the responses to decide what needs revisiting.

Record specific learning evidence and a brief evaluation for the Year 13 sessions.

Continue investigating group participation without assuming why students choose particular groups.

Wednesday – 21 October

Year 10 Science 1 – Lesson 3

The lesson went well overall. Students asked lots of questions during the paper. They also asked me to deliver part of the lesson in Hindi, which I did.

The paper took about five minutes longer than planned because of students’ questions. Including the test meant that the circuit activity was a little rushed, but we finished the entire lesson. My impression was that all students understood the circuit work.

We did not have time for students to write the exit ticket in their exercise books, so I used oral responses instead. This allowed us to finish the check, although I do not have an individual written response from every student to confirm my impression of whole-class understanding.

For the next delivery, allow more time for questions during the test and protect the practical activity time. Review the test papers and use a brief individual check next lesson to see whether any circuit ideas need revisiting.

Thursday – 22 October

Year 10 Science 2 – Lesson 4: informal feedback from my mentor teacher

My mentor teacher said the lesson went well and that students were engaged and learning. He noticed that I regularly ask whether students have any questions during and after teacher talk. He suggested keeping this, but occasionally following it with: “Okay, if nobody has a question, answer this then…” This would help check what students have actually understood and keep them thinking.

For the next lesson, prepare a few short questions linked to the learning intentions and use them after explanations, even when students have no questions. Use their responses to decide whether to move on or revisit an idea.

Friday – 23 October

Year 10 – Lesson 4: current and its measurement

The lesson went well. Students were able to work out and complete the practical task independently, and the discussions went well too. The planned activities finished about ten minutes early. I used the remaining time for students to complete an unfinished worksheet from an earlier lesson and to get to know them personally. This was a useful opportunity for relationship building.

For the next delivery, I need to review the timing and have a meaningful extension ready. Use a short fault-finding challenge: students inspect three circuit diagrams, identify which measures the lamp current, correct the faulty circuits and explain their reasoning. This applies their existing understanding of complete circuits and ammeter connections. A separate plan and slide deck have been prepared for the next delivery.

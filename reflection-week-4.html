Week 4 Reflection

Week 4 Reflection

19–23 October 2026

This reflection uses the “What? So what? Now what?” framework of Rolfe et al. (2001).

What?

This week I continued teaching the Year 10 electricity unit. In the lesson that included my pepeha, students asked many questions and requested that I deliver part of the lesson in Hindi, which I did. The assessment took about five minutes longer than planned, leaving the circuit activity rushed. We completed the lesson, but I used oral responses for the exit ticket instead of written answers.

In another lesson, students worked through the ammeter investigation independently and the discussions went well. They finished about ten minutes early. I used this time to complete an earlier worksheet and get to know them better. my mentor teacher’s informal feedback was encouraging: he felt students were engaged and learning. He suggested occasionally following “Any questions?” with a question of my own to check understanding.

I also observed the EOTC teacher managing the Year 7–8 beach investigation through measurement tasks, physical activity and calmly waiting for everyone’s attention. At Sea Dogs, my mentor teacher and the more experienced students supported beginners, including me.

So what?

The contrasting lesson timings showed me that a coherent plan still needs room for questions and differences in how quickly students work. Finishing an activity, or appearing engaged, does not by itself show that every student has understood. My oral exit questions gave me some useful information, but less evidence of individual understanding than a short written response would have. my mentor teacher’s suggestion reminded me that having no questions is not the same as understanding.

Sharing my identity and using the spare time for conversations created opportunities for relationship building. The outdoor experiences reinforced this: support from teachers and peers helped me participate as a beginner. the EOTC teacher’s patient approach also gave me a practical example of maintaining expectations without rushing or speaking over students.

I am making progress towards translating the curriculum into a unit and responsive lesson plans, constructing visual models, and learning to organise a local field trip. My experience of participating in trips has grown, but I still need to understand the safety procedures and documentation before taking a fuller planning role.

Now what?

For the next delivery, I have prepared a separate fault-finding challenge using circuit diagrams and concepts students already know. I will allow more time for questions while protecting the practical activity and a brief individual exit response. I will also prepare targeted questions after explanations and use students’ answers and marked work to decide what needs revisiting.

I will continue developing physical models with my mentor teacher and check their usefulness through students’ explanations and application of the concepts. I will discuss field-trip safety protocols, risk assessment and documentation with him so I can contribute more fully to planning a curriculum-linked local trip.

References

Rolfe, G., Freshwater, D., & Jasper, M. (2001). Critical reflection for nursing and the helping professions: A user’s guide. Palgrave.

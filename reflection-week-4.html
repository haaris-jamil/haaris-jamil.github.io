Week 4 Reflection

19–23 October 2026

This reflection follows Rolfe et al.’s (2001) “What? So what? Now what?” framework.

What?

This week, my Year 10 lessons had contrasting timings. In the lesson that included my pepeha, students asked questions and requested part of the teaching in Hindi. The assessment overran, leaving the circuit activity rushed, so I used oral exit responses instead of written answers.

In another lesson, students completed the ammeter investigation independently and finished ten minutes early. I used the time for an earlier worksheet and individual conversations. My mentor teacher suggested following some invitations for questions with a question that checks understanding.

I also observed the Years 7–8 beach investigation and joined Sea Dogs, where teachers and students supported me as a beginner.

So what?

These experiences helped me understand why even a carefully planned lesson needs flexibility. I want students to ask questions and explore ideas, but I also need to allow sufficient time for the practical work and for checking their understanding. The oral responses gave me some useful information, although they did not tell me what every student had understood. Black and Wiliam (2009) emphasise using evidence of understanding to decide what to do next, which is also the purpose of my mentor teacher’s suggestion about questioning.

Sharing my identity and talking individually created opportunities for relationships. Outdoors, I saw how patient expectations and peer support helped people participate. I am progressing with curriculum planning and physical models, but still need to understand the documentation behind field-trip organisation.

Now what?

I have prepared a fault-finding extension for early finishers. I will allow space for questions while protecting a brief individual check, and use responses to decide what needs revisiting. I will evaluate models through students’ explanations and discuss field-trip safety protocols, risk assessment and documentation with my mentor teacher.

References

Black, P., & Wiliam, D. (2009). Developing the theory of formative assessment. Educational Assessment, Evaluation and Accountability, 21(1), 5–31. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11092-008-9068-5

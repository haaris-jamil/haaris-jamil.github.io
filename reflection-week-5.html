Formal Reflection

Week 5 Reflection

26–30 October 2026 | Placement Week 5

This reflection follows Rolfe et al.’s (2001) “What? So what? Now what?” framework.

What?

This week, the main challenge was responding to different starting points while keeping the lessons moving. On Wednesday, the crossword revealed gaps in understanding, so I spent fifteen minutes revisiting earlier ideas. Students collected circuit readings, but graphing carried forward. On Thursday, most students worked productively, although I could not get two students to complete the task despite repeated reminders. On Friday, six boys who had missed the previous lesson needed substantial support. I worked through the experiment with them, then changed the planned resistance investigation into a demonstration to accommodate the remaining time.

So what?

These lessons reminded me that responding to learners involves more than adjusting timings. A recap can be worthwhile even when it delays the planned activity, but catching up one group can also reduce opportunities for everyone else. I think Friday’s discussion established useful connections between voltage, current and resistance, although this does not establish every student’s understanding. Repeated reminders were also insufficient for some students. my mentor teacher’s perspective on Thursday helped me recognise a positive in the interactions without overlooking the unfinished learning. I need to distinguish maintaining a relationship, securing participation and establishing understanding rather than treating them as the same outcome.

Now what?

I will prepare a short re-entry task for students who have missed a practical and agree with my mentor teacher how to support those who remain off task. I will retain brief individual checks so that class discussion is not my only evidence of understanding. I also want students to complete the hands-on resistance investigation, rather than leave the demonstration as their only experience. I will record these adaptations in the lesson plans while preserving the original unit plan. my mentor teacher’s formal feedback on Friday’s lesson is pending and will inform the next review.

References

Rolfe, G., Freshwater, D., & Jasper, M. (2001). Critical reflection for nursing and the helping professions: A user’s guide. Palgrave.

Rough Notes

Week 5 – Rough Notes

26–30 October 2026 | Placement Week 5

These notes retain the experiences and thoughts shared this week. They are a dated account, not a claim that every planned activity or learning outcome was achieved.

Monday – 26 October

Labour Day. No scheduled Year 10 lesson took place.

Tuesday – 27 October

No separate dated classroom observations were supplied for this day.

Wednesday – 28 October

Y10 Science 1 – Lesson 5: voltage and current investigation

The opening crossword was planned for five minutes but took about fifteen. Many learners had missed the previous lesson or did not have a secure enough understanding. I chose to revisit the underlying concepts in detail before moving on. I do not regret that decision: I wanted everyone to have at least a basic understanding from which to approach the new learning.

The circuit practical was messy, as expected, but almost everyone completed it properly and collected readings. The graph carried into the next lesson, which was an anticipated possibility. Practical completion alone does not establish individual understanding; the graphs and explanations still needed checking.

My visiting lecturer observed the lesson and we had a post-observation discussion. His formal report is a separate record. We subsequently revised plans to separate teacher and student activities and make the plenary more explicit. Curriculum statements were to remain verbatim and separate.

I did not agree with every feedback point. I felt I had used students’ names consistently. I also felt that the different meters and limited apparatus required individual group support and that a whole-class check could not resolve every group-specific problem. This is my interpretation, not a change to my visiting lecturer’s report. Progress checks can still help me identify where support is needed.

Thursday – 29 October

Y10 Science 2 – Lesson 4: current and its measurement

The lesson went quite well overall. Most students carried out the experiment productively. I did not manage to keep two students on task or get them to complete it. One continued eating despite reminders, while another wrote a song in his exercise book. Repeated redirection did not secure task completion, although both answered some questions.

I discussed this with my mentor teacher afterwards. He provided context about ongoing behaviour concerns and suggested that the interactions remaining non-abusive was a positive. This helped me put the experience into perspective without changing the fact that the task remained unfinished for these students. I need a more specific approach than repeated reminders alone.

Friday – 30 October

Y10 Science 1 – Lesson 6: graph interpretation and resistance

This was a difficult lesson. Six boys had missed the previous lesson and needed to be brought up to date. They were distracted and showed limited interest, and I struggled to keep them on task.

I seated them together at a central table and set up the earlier experiment for them. Even with this support, maintaining attention was difficult. Nevertheless, I worked through the experiment with them before moving on to the graph and the conclusions from the data.

I had to restructure the lesson as it unfolded. The resistance investigation intended as a student activity became a teacher demonstration because of time constraints. The planned hands-on activity was therefore not delivered as intended.

From the data, we identified that voltage divided by current was constant for the fixed component and called that constant resistance. We explored the relationship without using the term ‘proportional’, as students were not familiar with it. We then considered how changing resistance could change current and finished with examples.

I think I achieved the main conceptual learning objectives and did a reasonable job of managing a messy lesson. This is my assessment from the discussion, not evidence that every learner independently understood the relationship. I need to check individual understanding and revisit the hands-on investigation.

My mentor teacher formally observed this lesson. His written feedback is pending; no feedback or judgement from him has been invented here.

Planning decisions arising this week

Keep the original unit plan unchanged as evidence of initial intentions. Record responsive changes and the reasons for them in lesson plans and slides, and record actual delivery in the learning events register.

Prepare a manageable catch-up route after absence, protect an individual closing check, and discuss a specific response with my mentor teacher for learners who remain off task. These are next steps, not actions already evaluated.

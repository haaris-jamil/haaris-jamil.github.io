Week 5 Reflection

26–30 October 2026 | Placement Week 5

This reflection follows Rolfe et al.’s (2001) “What? So what? Now what?” framework.

What?

This week, I had to adapt my lessons to students who were at different points in their learning. On Wednesday, the crossword showed gaps in their understanding, and I spent about fifteen minutes revisiting the earlier ideas. The students collected circuit readings, but we had to carry the graphing forward. On Thursday, most worked productively, although two did not complete the task despite reminders. On Friday, six students who had missed the previous lesson needed considerable support. I worked through the experiment with them, and, since we were short of time, used the planned resistance investigation as a demonstration.

So what?

I realised that responding to students involves more than adjusting the time spent on an activity. Revisiting earlier ideas was necessary, but helping students who had missed a lesson also took time away from the rest of the class. I felt that Friday’s discussion helped connect voltage, current and resistance, although I still needed to check how well individual students understood those connections.

Black and Wiliam’s (2009) discussion of formative assessment helped me understand why it was useful to spend longer on the crossword: the students’ responses showed me what needed attention before we continued. However, I also need to hear from students who do not readily contribute to a discussion. My conversations with my mentor teacher reminded me that building relationships, encouraging participation and finding out what students understand are all important, but they do not necessarily happen together.

Now what?

I will prepare a short re-entry task for students who miss a practical and discuss how to follow up when reminders are ineffective. I will retain individual checks and give students an opportunity to complete the hands-on resistance investigation. I will document adaptations while preserving the original unit plan. At this point, Friday’s formal mentor feedback is pending and will inform my next review.

References

Black, P., & Wiliam, D. (2009). Developing the theory of formative assessment. Educational Assessment, Evaluation and Accountability, 21(1), 5–31. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11092-008-9068-5

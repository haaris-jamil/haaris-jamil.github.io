Lesson 03: Energy supply and two-switch circuit challenges

Year 10 Science 1 · 21 Oct 2026 · Completed

Inquiry question What does the cell supply, and what controls a lamp in a one-loop circuit?

Whakataukī

Whāia te mātauranga hei oranga mō koutou.

Meaning: Seek knowledge for the wellbeing of all.

Why this lesson: Connects the pepeha and electricity-access context to investigating circuits through predictions, evidence and careful claims.

Curriculum alignment

Knowledge

  • Electric circuits provide a continuous path that allows charge to move through components.
  • Energy is transferred between components in a circuit as electric charge moves and interacts with those components.

Practices

  • Construct and test series and parallel circuits to observe current flow and circuit function.
  • Predict and test how adding or removing components affects current in series and parallel circuits.
  • Use appropriate scientific conventions when reporting about electricity.

Learning intention

We are learning to explain the cell's energy-supplying role and investigate, represent and explain a complete circuit.

Success criteria

  • I can use the ramp model to explain qualitatively what the cell supplies to charge.
  • I can test two switch positions and all four two-switch combinations, then justify a rule from evidence.

I can draw my one-cell, one-lamp, two-switch circuit in my exercise book using standard symbols.

Resources

  • One low-voltage cell/holder, one lamp/holder, TWO switches and sufficient leads per pair; pre-tested spare kit. Label switches S1 and S2. No second lamp is needed. Secure ramp, identical balls and a return/lifting route for the energy model.
  • Optional teacher-only materials samples are not needed for this class; the planned challenge uses the existing one-lamp kit.

Embedded pepeha; unfinished Lesson 2 worksheet for a short recap; exercise books, pens and circuit-symbol reference. The final five-question exit ticket is displayed on screen and answered with one word per item in exercise books; no printed ticket.

Safety

Secure the ramp and keep balls contained. Use low-voltage cells only. Disconnect the cell before moving switches/cell or changing connections. Keep one conducting loop, with both switches inserted into that loop. Never bridge the lamp, short the cell, or introduce branches.

Teaching and learning sequence

Time and phase

Teacher actions

Student actions

Assessment and responsive support

0–15 min

Pepeha and connection to electricity

Present the embedded pepeha: 10 minutes for places and people, 3 for Purkal and electricity access, 2 for questions and transition.

Listen to the introduction and ask brief questions.

Notice connections and questions without adding a worksheet during the introduction.

15–30 min

Recap, ramp model and demonstration

15–18: direct completion of priority Lesson 2 items; 18–20: check the key charge explanation. 20–25: recall ramp/balls, lifting as energy supplied to charge, descent as energy transfer at a lamp, and model limitations. Retain the existing qualitative terminology. 25–30: demonstrate a safe one-lamp circuit.

Finish priority recap items, explain charge, relate the ramp to a circuit, and trace the demonstrated loop.

Use the recap to identify charge misconceptions; check the complete path before practical work.

30–51 min

Predict, test and challenge circuit claims

30–34: support the starting one-cell, one-lamp, one-switch loop. 34–41: set the switch-location challenge. 41–47: guide the two-switch challenge in the same loop, including all four combinations and component relocation. 47–51: require a labelled circuit drawing.

Predict and test each challenge, swap roles and explain the complete path. Derive a rule from results. Draw the tested circuit with standard symbols and trace it to a partner.

Elicit evidence before consolidating the rule. Check drawings and explanations individually; retain the drawing task.

51–60 min

Consolidation, quick check and pack-up / Plenary

Plenary (51–56): revisit the learning intention and display the existing five short exit questions. Pack-up (56–60): direct disconnection and return of kits, then inspect responses.

Write numbers 1–5 and one-word answers in exercise books. Disconnect and return equipment safely.

Check individual responses for complete-path and switch misconceptions. Note what to revisit next lesson; use the existing exit task rather than adding another worksheet.

Hear each learner's ramp-model explanation, switch prediction and evidence-based rule. Check their two-switch circuit diagram in exercise books. Display-only exit ticket (5 minutes): 1 What does the cell supply to charge? 2 Which term describes the cell's energy supply? 3 Lamp on: both switches must be ____. 4 Lamp on: the conducting path must be ____. 5 Moving the cell along this loop changes the rule: yes/no? Teacher-only key: energy; EMF; closed; complete (accept unbroken/closed); no. No answers appear on the learner slide.

Use the short recap to check retained charge ideas. During the challenge, elicit each learner's prediction before testing; ask for a complete-path explanation and a counterexample to an overgeneralised claim. Listen for treating the switch as acting only on a nearby component. Use the existing four-item Quick check at 51–55 minutes; no extra worksheet.

Support: a labelled kit, one verified working starting loop, role swaps, oral predictions and a circuit-symbol reference. Each partner must explain and draw; do not let one do all construction. Extension: evaluate whether a closed switch guarantees a lit lamp and propose another possible break without being shown the solution. Do not demonstrate the two-switch result before students test. The ramp is an energy analogy: gravity drives balls, an electric field drives charge; the cell's chemical processes transfer energy. Balls can roll down an isolated ramp, unlike sustained current which requires a complete path. Height difference maps to energy supplied per unit charge qualitatively; no equations or units are assessed.

My mentor teacher taught Lesson 2 to this group. Offer equipment-layout prompts and a working reference kit where needed. If basic construction is insecure, support the switch-position comparison before the counterexample. Extension: evaluate the claim that the lamp should keep glowing while electrons 'on their way' arrive after the switch opens. Use the complete-path requirement; do not expect a quantitative explanation of switching transients. Prioritise explanations over extra building.

Questions to guide discussion

  • What in the ramp model represents the cell supplying energy, and what represents energy being transferred at the lamp?
  • What rule fits all four combinations of S1 and S2? Does changing their locations or the cell's location change your evidence?
  • How can your circuit diagram and tracing of the complete path support your rule?

Post lesson evaluation

Delivery reflection - Y10 Science 1, 21 October 2026

Delivered 21 October 2026. Students asked many questions during the paper and requested delivery in Hindi, which I provided. The paper ran about five minutes over. The circuit activity was rushed, but the entire lesson was completed. My impression was that all students understood the circuit work. Exit-ticket questions were answered orally instead of in exercise books; no individual written exit responses were collected.

The lesson went well overall. The test and additional questions reduced practical time. For the next delivery, allow more time for questions while protecting the circuit activity and a brief individual check. Review the test papers and check individual circuit understanding next lesson; oral whole-class responses alone do not establish that every student has understood.

GOAL 1: CURRICULUM, UNIT AND LESSON PLANNING

Goal

Develop, teach and evaluate a coherent Science unit that translates curriculum requirements into sequenced lessons responsive to learners’ needs.

Rationale

I have substantial experience teaching Physics, but need to develop my understanding of planning within the New Zealand curriculum and responding to learners in this setting.

Action steps

1. Review the curriculum, class information and unit sequence with my mentor teacher, and identify appropriate learning intentions and assessment.

2. Teach the agreed sequence to both Year 10 groups, using class-specific assessment and mentor feedback to adjust subsequent lessons.

3. Evaluate the unit using student-learning evidence and my mentor teacher’s feedback at the end of placement.

Success criteria

1. My unit and lesson plans show clear curriculum alignment and a coherent progression, reviewed with my mentor teacher.

2. I teach the agreed sequence, record evidence-informed adjustments and evaluate learner outcomes alongside mentor feedback.

Midpoint evaluation

I have developed a Year 10 electricity unit from the curriculum and am currently delivering it. After each lesson, I reflect on student responses, learning and pacing, and adjust subsequent lessons accordingly. My next step is to use assessment evidence more systematically to evaluate the unit’s effectiveness.

Final evaluation and next steps

Links to professional standards / KTT

Standard 6 - Teaching: delivery, assessment and adaptation.

Standard 2 - Professional learning: using reflection and mentor feedback to develop practice.

GOAL 2: VISUAL AND WORKING MODELS

Goal

Design and construct visual and working models, use them in teaching, and evaluate how they support students’ understanding of scientific concepts.

Rationale

Physical models can make abstract scientific ideas easier to investigate and discuss. I want to construct resources that serve a clear learning purpose and examine their usefulness in practice.

Action steps

1. Construct or co-construct at least two appropriate models with my mentor teacher’s guidance, documenting my contribution and each resource’s purpose.

2. Use completed models in teaching and explicitly explain the analogy and its limitations.

3. Collect students’ explanations or other learning evidence and use this, alongside my mentor teacher’s feedback, to evaluate and refine the models.

Success criteria

1. I construct or co-construct at least two resources and document my contribution and their intended learning purpose.

2. I use the resources in teaching, explain their limitations and evaluate students’ understanding using recorded evidence. Unfinished resources are identified as work in progress.

Midpoint evaluation

I have constructed and used physical models, including a ramp model to explain voltage. I am discussing further designs with my mentor and aim to build a few more before my placement ends. I will evaluate their effectiveness through students’ explanations and application of the concepts.

Final evaluation and next steps

Links to professional standards / KTT

Standard 5 - Design for learning: selecting and constructing representations for learners’ needs.

Standard 6 - Teaching: using models and checking understanding.

Standard 2 - Professional learning: evaluating and refining the resources through evidence and feedback.

Evidence

Evaluation of each model.

Photographic evidence

Ball-track construction: I built or helped build these energy-transformation tracks, including a 360° loop. This supports Goal 2’s construction criterion. Learner explanations are still needed to evaluate their effectiveness.

Large ramp: a model I built or helped build and used as an analogy for voltage. This documents the resource; lesson reflections and learner responses are needed to evaluate understanding and the analogy’s limitations.

GOAL 3: LOCAL FIELD TRIP

Goal

Plan, organise and evaluate a curriculum-linked local field trip, working collaboratively with my mentor and relevant school or community partners.

Rationale

A local field trip can connect classroom Science with electricity generation in the community. Planning it will develop my ability to coordinate an accessible learning experience with colleagues and relevant partners.

Action steps

1. Develop the beach investigation with my mentor teacher, choosing a feasible wind- or water-powered generator model and a clear link to the electricity unit.

2. Coordinate the required school approval, supervision, consent, equipment and access arrangements, including a suitable alternative where needed.

3. Prepare linked learning activities, help deliver the trip and evaluate students’ learning and the experience with my mentor teacher.

Success criteria

1. I secure the required approval and arrangements and prepare activities with a clear curriculum purpose and appropriate support for participation.

2. I help deliver the trip, collect student-learning evidence and evaluate the experience with my mentor teacher, recording improvements for future planning.

Midpoint evaluation

I have participated in four field trips, with another coming up, and observed how teachers manage learning, engagement and behaviour outside the classroom. I still need to develop my understanding of the planning requirements. My next step is to work with my mentor on safety protocols, risk assessment and documentation for a curriculum-linked local trip.

Final evaluation and next steps

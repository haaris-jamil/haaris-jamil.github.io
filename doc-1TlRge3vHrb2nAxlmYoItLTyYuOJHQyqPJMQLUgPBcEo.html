Professional Experience 1

Lesson 09: How do we generate electricity?

Year 10 Science 1 · 11 Nov 2026 · Planned

Year 10 Science | 55 minutes

Learning intention

Use practical observations to explain how movement can produce a voltage.

Success criteria

  • Record what changes across two or more trials at each practical setup.
  • Use the opened motor to connect observations from the moving magnet and the hand-turned motor.
  • Use one observation to explain part of how a wind turbine generates electricity.

Preparation

Set up and test two oscillating-magnet and coil stations with voltmeters, two DC motors connected to voltmeters, and one opened, unpowered motor. Mark each station A, B or D to match the worksheet. Secure the magnet and motor apparatus and check the meter response before class.

Arrange tables away from the benches for pair discussions. Print one double-sided worksheet per pair.

Whakataukī: E koekoe te tūī, e ketekete te kākā, e kūkū te kererū. The tūī chatters, the kākā squawks, and the kererū coos. Read it aloud. Ask pairs why the saying gives each bird a different sound and what that might say about people. Hear two interpretations.

Teaching sequence

Teacher activity

Student activity

Arrival and whakataukī | 7 min Play Tour a Wind Turbine with sound as students arrive. https://youtu.be/5vj6GwVhQT0 Pause the video, lead the whakataukī and hand out the worksheets.

Watch the turbine tour while settling. Read the whakataukī and collect a worksheet.

Station investigation | 33 min Direct pairs to free setups. Form one trio if needed. Check how students are using the apparatus and listen to their initial explanations. Ask “What did you see that makes you think that?”

Visit A, B and D. Use either copy of A or B. At each bench, do the test or sketch, note what you see, reset the equipment and move away to discuss and write. Choose another station if one is busy.

Connect observations | 5 min Bring pairs back to their worksheets and invite them to compare A, B and D.

After visiting all three station types, use your sketch of the opened motor to answer the two comparison questions.

Plenary | 10 min Give pairs two minutes to prepare a Te Uku explanation. Hear two pairs and ask the class which observations support each explanation. Use up to one minute to clarify the relationship between movement and voltage if needed. Collect individual responses.

Explain what the wind does at a Te Uku turbine that your hand did at B. Use A or D as evidence. Individually name one setup that produced a voltage and give one observation to support your answer.

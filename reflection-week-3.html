Week 3 Reflection

12–16 October 2026

Reflection model: Rolfe et al.’s (2001) What? So what? Now what? model

This week, my teaching focused on responding to students’ understanding and helping them reason for themselves. I try to approach situations without fixed expectations. During the introductory Year 10 lessons, students’ responses showed that basic charge ideas needed attention. I therefore revised the sequence so that Lesson 2 developed the atomic model and static electricity before moving to circuits.

The lesson felt coherent, and students were engaged in the models and demonstrations. Some initially did not want to participate, but later wanted to complete their worksheets and asked for help. Rather than give them answers, I explained the underlying principles so that they could work out their own responses. Their willingness to seek help showed participation, although it did not establish independent understanding.

This connects with scaffolding: support can enable learners to tackle a task they cannot yet manage unaided (Wood et al., 1976). For me, the important question is whether that support helps students explain the science themselves. Compared with senior Physics, junior Science feels less academically intensive and less driven by syllabus coverage. There is more space to develop students’ thinking, while retaining clear scientific learning goals.

The unfinished worksheets exposed a limitation in my timing and left the written evidence incomplete. I will begin Lesson 3 with five minutes to finish them, then check explanations before introducing circuits. I will also use coloured, labelled hats to clarify the particle roles in the second delivery. My next step is to distinguish engagement, supported success and independent understanding more carefully, using students’ explanations to decide when to progress.

Rough notes

Week 3 – Rough notes

12–16 October 2026

Notes compiled from what I shared during the week. Dates are used where established; undated observations and meeting outcomes are kept separately. These are working notes, not a record of every activity.

Monday – 12 October

First Y10 Science 2 lesson after the break. my mentor teacher led most of it; he had not yet gone through my unit or lesson plan and followed his own sequence.

Covered current, voltage and resistance; sources such as solar, wind and hydro; devices and uses such as fridges and mowers; circuit symbols representing real circuits.

I took over for about fifteen minutes. Van de Graaff demonstration; used the atomic model to introduce charging by friction.

Students showed little existing knowledge about charges. Many did not know that electrons are negative. Need to establish fundamentals before progressing.

Original static-electricity outcomes moved into Lesson 2. Circuit construction moves to Lesson 3.

My mentor teacher subsequently suggested repeating this introduction with the other Year 10 group, then using my planned charge lesson. He also suggested a worksheet. This advice came between the two Lesson 1 deliveries.

Tuesday – 13 October

No separate classroom event was specifically dated to Tuesday in my notes.

Planning work discussed this week: keep Lesson 2 coherent; allow at least fifteen minutes for my pepeha; teach the relevant ideas before asking students to explain demonstrations.

Use the Van de Graaff again for explanation: paper on the charged dome flying off, then sparks to the earthed sphere. The spark introduces charge moving.

Do not repeat the rubbed plastic rod lifting paper or the hand-crank generator as introductory demonstrations; these have already been seen.

Magnets and sealed iron filings can make attraction and repulsion visible. Be clear that magnetic poles are an analogy, not electric charges; filings show magnetic fields.

Use volunteers to model the atom, with protons and neutrons in the nucleus and electrons around it. Count balanced charge, then add or remove electrons while leaving the nucleus unchanged. Electrons stand; they do not orbit. Roles do not need to be assigned by gender.

My concern: predictions and discussion tasks need a purpose and sufficient prior knowledge. An explanation of a familiar demonstration makes more sense than predicting something students have already watched.

Wednesday – 14 October

Lesson 1 with Y10 Science 1 was broadly a repeat of the introductory lesson with Y10 Science 2.

My reflection remains similar for both groups: students need the charge basics established before more advanced applications. One shared reflection is used, with separate dated register entries.

During Year 13 Physics, worked one-to-one with a student through a past paper. I knew beforehand that the session would happen, but did not review questions or prepare a specific lesson plan. Most decisions were made on the spot while guiding the student through solving the questions.

Recorded as responsive one-to-one exam support. Whether it meets the “planned and led” requirement is unconfirmed; excluded from the required total pending clarification.

Thursday – 15 October

Delivered Lesson 2 to Y10 Science 2. I felt it went almost perfectly.

Sequence: pepeha and personal context, student atom model, magnets/iron-filings analogy, then Van de Graaff paper repulsion and sparks.

Some students initially did not want to engage. Towards the end they wanted to complete their worksheets and asked for help.

When helping, I tried not to give answers. I explained the underlying principles so students could work out the answers themselves.

Students did not have enough time to finish the worksheets. Need to avoid treating engagement as proof of independent understanding.

Plan: first five minutes of Lesson 3 to complete the unfinished sheets, then a short answer check and any necessary reteaching before circuits.

Informal mentor comments reported at the time: my mentor teacher felt the lesson went very well; students were thinking and engaged. Suggested coloured hats to identify the electron, proton and neutron roles. These comments are rough-note context; formal feedback is kept separate from my published reflection.

For the second group, retain the sequence and add different coloured hats with written particle labels.

Lesson 3 revised: recap first; bridge from a brief spark to sustained charge flow; demonstrate and teach circuit symbols before students build a working circuit; materials testing follows if time permits. No unsupported prediction tasks.

Junior Science feels “a lot less academically intensive” and less about ticking boxes for syllabus completion, with more emphasis on getting students to think on their own. This is a difference in emphasis, not an absence of scientific goals.

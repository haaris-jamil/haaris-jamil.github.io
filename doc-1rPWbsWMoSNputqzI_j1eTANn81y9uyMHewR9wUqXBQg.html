Professional Experience 1

Lesson 07: Reliable connections and switched outlets

Year 10 Science 2 · 9 Nov 2026 · Planned

Full lesson plan | Year 10 Science | Teacher: Haaris Jamil | Duration: 60 minutes

Class: Y10 Science 2 Date: 9 November 2026 - planned Period / room: as confirmed timetable

Lesson purpose and prior learning

This lesson applies students’ previous circuit work to a familiar household fitting. I will use the difficulties encountered during practical work to explore why reliable connections matter. Students will identify active, neutral and protective earth, connect a supply cable to a factory-wired switched outlet and check its operation using an isolated 12 V AC supply. My focus is on how different levels of support help each learner participate and explain the completed circuit.

Whakataukī

He manako te kōura i kore ai.

Meaning: Wishing for crayfish alone brings none. Practical effort is needed to achieve a result.

Justification: Students turn their ideas about failed circuits into practical action by making, checking and correcting connections.

Curriculum alignment

Knowledge

  • Electric circuits provide a continuous path that allows charge to move through components.
  • Conductors (e.g. metals) allow the movement of electric charge.
  • Insulators (e.g. plastic) restrict the movement of electric charge.

Practices

  • Construct and test series and parallel circuits to observe current flow and circuit function.
  • Use appropriate scientific conventions when reporting about electricity.

Learning intention

We are learning to make and check reliable connections to a switched outlet and explain the functions of active, neutral and protective earth.

Success criteria

  • I can identify active/live, neutral and protective earth on the actual fitting and explain their household functions.
  • I can identify A, N and E, connect a supply cable to the outlet model and explain which internal path the switch interrupts.
  • I can inspect connections and use disconnected continuity results to identify a fault or justify a correction.
  • I can explain why the outlet switch interrupts the active path while neutral and earth remain continuous.

Resources

Factory-wired switched outlets; compatible cable; one 12 V AC-rated lamp and suitable holder per test arrangement; screwdrivers, strippers and cutters; continuity meters; protected connectors and enclosures; the Lesson 7 worksheet and diagram. An annotated example, vocabulary prompts and additional practice cable provide optional support.

Functional tests require a safety-isolated 12 V AC school supply with output current limiting or fusing appropriate to the lamp, cable and fitting.

Before the lesson, I will test the outlet circuit, verify the supply protection and lamp rating, and check the switched active path and continuous neutral/earth paths by disconnected continuity testing. The demonstration and annotated example will use the same fitting as the students’ task.

A completed outlet example will support the demonstration. A disconnected fault example will provide an extension once the core task is checked. I will arrange the testing station so that groups can be checked as they become ready.

Safety and organisation

Students will prepare, connect, inspect and modify their assemblies with the supply disconnected. I will inspect each assembly before controlling its connection to the 12 V AC supply. Conducting parts will be enclosed or otherwise protected during powered testing. Each assembly will be disconnected before it is returned to students for handling or adjustment.

I will use the supply’s verified isolation and appropriately rated output protection, secure the fittings and supervise tools and offcut collection. All construction and corrections will take place with the supply disconnected. I will retain control of powered testing and disconnect each assembly before students handle it.

Assemblies will be labelled “12 V AC teaching model only”, kept separate from equipment in mains service and retained or dismantled at school. Student assemblies will have no mains supply plug. Powered testing will take place once the supply, protected connections and compatible load have been checked; disconnected inspection and continuity testing provide the alternative where these requirements cannot be met.

The fixture terminals retain their household labels A, N and E. The isolated 12 V AC output has two secondary terminals rather than household active and neutral conductors. Earth continuity will be checked separately. The model demonstrates connections and switching while household conductor functions are explained qualitatively.

Teaching and learning sequence

Teacher activity and timing

Student activity

0–3 min | Reconnect with previous practical work

I will ask students to identify possible causes of the earlier circuit difficulties and suggest checks. I will use their responses to identify gaps in complete-path reasoning and relate reliable connections to everyday use.

Students will record two possible causes, discuss a check with a partner and share their reasoning.

3–8 min | Conductors and household roles

I will introduce the whakataukī and explain A (active/live), N (neutral) and E (protective earth). I will trace the switched active path and continuous neutral/earth paths in the outlet model.

Students will match functions, identify terminal markings and trace the paths on the diagram. They will distinguish the fixture’s labels from the isolated supply outputs.

8–13 min | Demonstrate the connection task

I will model cable preparation and a checked connection using the actual fitting. I will show the inspection points and compare the enclosed switch with the familiar knife switch. I will check initial understanding before pairs begin.

Students will identify the conducting parts and insulation, locate terminals and explain one quality check. Each pair will agree who makes the first connection and when roles change.

13–38 min | Paired construction and responsive support

I will circulate, check each student’s first preparation or connection and use targeted questions. Learners needing support will receive the annotated example and a first-connection check. Ready pairs will work from the diagram and complete the extension after checking their core task.

Students will connect the supply cable to the factory-wired switched outlet while disconnected. Both partners will practise. They will inspect their work and make corrections before requesting a teacher check.

38–48 min | Inspection and 12 V AC testing

I will inspect completed assemblies and supervise disconnected continuity checks. I will control the protected 12 V AC functional tests. I will compare students’ explanations and checks, not only whether a lamp operates.

Students will present their checks, trace the switched active path and observe the lamp in both switch positions. They will record results individually. Waiting pairs will annotate the paths or complete the disconnected diagnostic task.

48–54 min | Individual plenary

I will revisit the learning intention and ask each learner to identify the interrupted conductor, explain one quality check and explain why switching only neutral would leave a household load connected to active.

Students will complete the brief exit responses independently. They may give an oral explanation where a writing barrier would obscure their understanding.

54–60 min | Review and pack-up

I will briefly address misconceptions identified in the exit responses, record support needed for the next lesson and disconnect the supply. I will supervise collection of assemblies, tools and offcuts.

Students will submit the worksheet and pack away. They will leave all labelled teaching assemblies with the teacher.

Assessment and differentiation

I will use the opening responses to identify starting points and provide support accordingly. During construction I will record each learner’s contribution, check the first connection and ask them to explain a selected path. Inspection records, continuity results and individual exit responses will provide evidence of understanding. A working lamp alone will not establish each student’s learning.

Differentiation will retain the same core task and success criteria. Students needing support will use the annotated terminal example, vocabulary prompts, additional practice cable and a first-connection check. I will break the task into manageable stages and offer a demonstration at the point of difficulty. Students ready to work more independently will use the basic diagram and then explain or diagnose a disconnected fault. Both partners will alternate making and checking connections; individual questions and exit responses will establish each learner’s understanding.

I will recognise differing starting points, provide support that enables participation and reduce that support as learners become more independent. I will check both partners' contributions, use questions to reveal individual understanding and offer an extension that deepens learning for students who finish early.

Inspection checklist

  • Connections match the checked diagram and actual terminal markings.
  • Conductors are securely retained using the component manufacturer’s method.
  • Insulation remains intact; exposed conductor is appropriately contained and stray strands are controlled.
  • Cable support, enclosure and covers are suitable for the test arrangement.
  • The assembly is disconnected and ready for inspection and continuity testing.

Scientific understanding

Loose or high-resistance connections can cause local heating, while an interrupted path can prevent operation. The earlier circuit failures remain unexplained. Students will use inspection and disconnected continuity checks to investigate possible faults. Lesson 7 worksheet: View portfolio evidence

A denotes active (live), N denotes neutral and E denotes protective earth. In the single-pole outlet used for this model, the switch interrupts the active path while neutral and earth remain continuous. Interrupting only neutral would stop normal load current while leaving parts connected to active. Active and neutral carry the normal household load current; protective earth provides a fault path. Neutral remains potentially hazardous. Common identifies a changeover-switch contact and is distinct from neutral.

Plenary: individual checks

  • Identify A, N and E and the conductor interrupted by the outlet switch.
  • Name one connection-quality check and the problem it helps identify.
  • Explain why interrupting only neutral would leave a household load connected to active.

Timing and carryover

The timings allow one core practical and an individual plenary. Begin by checking the actual stopping point from Science 2 Lesson 6 on 5 November. Complete any essential graph or resistance carryover before the outlet task and carry unfinished construction forward. Preserve safe testing and pack-up. The parallel-lamp investigation follows in Science 2 Lesson 8.

Evidence of learning

Evidence will include anonymised practical photographs, individual checks, corrected connections, the worksheet and exit responses. I will record the support provided and how learners responded to it. Formal mentor feedback will remain separate from my post-lesson reflection.

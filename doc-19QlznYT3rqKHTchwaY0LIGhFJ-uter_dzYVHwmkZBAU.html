Professional Experience 1

Lesson 10: Local beach electricity-generation investigation

Year 10 Science 2 · 19 Nov 2026 · Completed

Year 10 Science 2 | Thursday 19 November 2026 | 35 minutes at the beach

Learning intention

Test the turbines students built and use the results to explain what makes a good turbine design and what they would improve.

Timing and activities

Before the investigation: 5 minutes to assemble and organise the group, then about 10 minutes to walk to the beach. Allow about 10 minutes for the walk back.

0–5 min | Arrive and set up. Reconfirm the agreed boundaries. Check the turbines and show students how to count the lit LEDs on the shared circuit board.

5–27 min | Build and test. Set up the wind turbines and test them directly in the available wind. For the water turbines, build a small contained reservoir in the approved area and release carried water through an outlet onto the rotor. Take turns using the same LED board. Record how many LEDs light up for each trial, including zero. Note calm conditions or changes in water flow; a low count may reflect the conditions as well as the design.

27–35 min | Reflect and pack up. Discuss: What made a design work well? What would you change and why? Ask each group to use one observation or LED count in its answer. Collect equipment and leave the area as found.

Equipment and safety

Bring the wind and water turbine models, one shared LED circuit board, materials for a small contained reservoir and outlet, carried water, a collection tray, and a simple recording sheet. Follow the school’s approved field-trip arrangements. No water entry or damming natural waterways. The teacher checks the rotors and handles the LED board and connections, which stay dry. Keep hands clear of moving blades.

Use the LED count as a comparison of the models under the conditions tested. Do not measure voltage. If there is no usable wind, record that limitation rather than treating it as a design failure.

Lesson reflection 19 November 2026

What?

The lesson did not go perfectly, but I felt it was a suitable final lesson with this group. The time available was too short to build a proper reservoir. The windmills worked well, but the reservoir for the water turbines did not work as intended. We adapted by using a nearby hose, which allowed us to test the water turbines.

So what?

We did not achieve everything as planned, but we overcame difficulties and found an alternative way to continue the investigation. I felt this made a suitable farewell to the group. The experience also showed me that the reservoir construction needed more time than I had allowed.

Now what?

For a similar investigation, I would allow more time to build and test the reservoir, or prepare part of it beforehand. I would also have an alternative water source ready so students could still test their turbines if the reservoir did not work.

Mentor feedback

My mentor described the field trip as the “icing on the cake” for the electricity unit. He highlighted its real-life challenges, opportunities for students to express their understanding and ideas, and further relationship-building. He identified planning, enthusiasm for the topic, challenge and creativity as strengths. He suggested that a two-hour session would allow students to build a dam, and that smaller groups would give them more opportunity to share ideas.

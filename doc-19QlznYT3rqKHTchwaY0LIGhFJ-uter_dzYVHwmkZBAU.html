Lesson 11: Local beach electricity-generation investigation

Inquiry question How can moving air or falling water generate electricity in a small model?

Classes

Y10 Science 2

Date

TBC - planned; date and visit arrangements to be agreed

Suggested duration

60 minutes of learning plus walking time – agree a suitable visit window with my mentor teacher

Lesson

11 of 12

Unit

Electricity and circuits

Teacher

Haaris

Whakataukī

Toitū te marae a Tāne, toitū te marae a Tangaroa, toitū te iwi.

Meaning: If land and sea are sustained, the people will prosper.

Why this lesson: Connects the beach investigation with caring for land and sea while exploring electricity generation.

Curriculum alignment

Knowledge

  • Multiple methods are used to generate electricity (e.g. turbines, solar panels). Note: In-depth understanding of electromagnetic induction is not expected at this level.
  • Energy is transferred between components in a circuit as electric charge moves and interacts with those components.

Practices

  • Measure current and voltage in different parts of a circuit using appropriate instruments.
  • Research methods and implications of electricity generation (e.g. turbines, solar panels) and evaluate their relevance to New Zealand.

Learning intention

We are learning to use a small wind- or water-driven generator to investigate electricity generation and explain the energy transfers.

Success criteria

  • I can describe the energy-transfer chain from wind or stored water to the turbine/generator and electrical output.
  • I can measure and record a voltage with units, stating the test conditions and limitations.
  • I can use evidence to evaluate the model’s output and relate it to electricity generation in New Zealand.

Resources

  • Bench-tested small DC motor used as a generator; suitable rotor/water wheel and secure coupling. Confirm a measurable output and meter range with my mentor teacher before the visit.
  • Investigation sheet with prediction, setup sketch, conditions, repeated voltage readings and conclusion; generation comparison organiser
  • Wind option: securely mounted small rotors, protected connectors, DC voltmeter/multimeter and optional wind indicator. Water option: contained tray/sand-reservoir model, carried water, pipe/nozzle, wheel/generator and collection container. Keep the motor/meter dry.
  • Clipboards, pencils, school-approved recording equipment if needed; agreed walking route and visit arrangements

Safety

Obtain school EOTC approval and follow the site-specific risk assessment, supervision and consent arrangements. Confirm the beach/site and route with my mentor teacher; check weather, tide, boundaries and accessibility. Work in the approved area away from surf and water entry. Protect hands from moving rotors and keep meters/motors/connectors dry. Use a contained water model; do not dam natural waterways or alter habitat. Take materials away after use. Provide an equivalent school-based fan/water-tray investigation if access, weather or learner needs prevent participation.

Teaching and learning sequence

Time and phase

Teacher actions

Student actions

Assessment and responsive support

8 min

Preparation at school

Confirm the bench-tested wind or contained-water option with my mentor teacher and explain the investigation conditions and energy-transfer focus.

Predict the energy-transfer chain and identify what conditions to record.

Check readiness, safe method and the limitations of varying wind or water flow.

8 min

Model setup

Use the separately agreed walking allocation; establish site boundaries and roles, check secure rotors and dry/protected meters, and oversee setup. Site, date, route and option remain subject to agreement.

Follow the agreed route, boundaries and roles; help set up the approved model.

Check access, supervision and equipment readiness before investigation.

22 min

Investigate

Supervise repeated voltage readings. Wind: prompt orientation and wind-condition records. Water: supervise the contained carried-water model and selected heights with motor, wheel and outlet fixed.

Measure and record voltage and conditions; compare trials and retain zero readings as evidence.

Check comparability and limitations; natural wind is variable. Keep motor and meters dry and follow the existing EOTC arrangements.

15 min

Explain and compare

Guide the energy-transfer explanation and comparison with another New Zealand generation method; connect to Purkal's electricity access.

Draw the chain, use readings to explain findings and identify model limits.

Check that students distinguish voltage from power and avoid assuming the small model could supply a home.

7 min

Reflect and collect / Plenary

Plenary and pack-up (7 min): reserve about 4 minutes for a claim, supporting reading, limitation and improvement; summarise learning, then direct equipment collection and site care. Walking and extended cleanup are allocated separately.

Write the brief evidence-based conclusion, submit it, then pack away and leave the site as found.

Review energy-transfer chains, measurement conditions and limitations. Use findings to decide what needs consolidating back at school.

Assessment for learning

Predictions, setup sketches, repeated voltage readings with conditions, energy-transfer diagram and a conclusion acknowledging limits. A zero reading is valid evidence to discuss, not an automatic failure.

Support and extension

Provide a labelled model, clear roles, vocabulary and a partially completed results table. Include an accessible school-based alternative. Extend by keeping a fixed load and measuring voltage/current to calculate output power; open-circuit voltage alone is not a measure of power.

Questions to guide discussion

  • What changes from wind or a raised water reservoir through the turbine and generator?
  • What did the meter measure, and were the conditions comparable between trials?
  • What can this model tell us about real generation, and what can it not establish?

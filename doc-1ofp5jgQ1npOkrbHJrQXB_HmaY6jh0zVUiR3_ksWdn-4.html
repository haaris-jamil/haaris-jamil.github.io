Sound interference and wavelength

Learning-event plan | Planned 2 November 2026 | Placement Week 6

Context

Teacher: Haaris Jamil. Year 13 Physics, Period 3, 11:20–12:20, planned 2 November 2026. Allow approximately 35–40 minutes within the period; confirm with my mentor teacher. Haaris has constructed a two-speaker signal-generator setup.

Assessment-standard links

Year 13: AS 91523, Demonstrate understanding of wave systems.

Learning intention and success criteria

Use two coherent sound sources to explain interference and estimate wavelength from a measured pattern. Students identify maxima/minima, explain them through superposition and path difference, record geometry with units, calculate a wavelength where the method is valid, and identify one limitation.

Preparation, resources and safety

Use two similar speakers driven by the same sinusoidal signal with matched polarity and an appropriate amplifier if required. Check generator/amplifier load compatibility before connecting speakers. Position the speakers at equal height and mark their centre-to-centre separation d. Mark a straight measurement line parallel to the source baseline at perpendicular distance D from its midpoint. Pretest a repeatable pattern at comfortable sound level; keep exposure brief and offer a visual/microphone alternative for hearing or sensory needs. Keep leads secured and the walking route clear. Do not increase loudness to compensate for a poor pattern.

Resources: constructed setup, generator and suitable drive arrangement, tape measures, masking tape, metre rule, calculator and results sheet/books. A microphone and level display are useful if available; keep its height and orientation fixed. Record bench-test settings before delivery: frequency ____ Hz; d ____ m; D ____ m; repeatable maxima/minima ____; sound-level check ____; my mentor teacher check ____.

Scientific model and measurement decisions

For in-phase sources, maxima occur at path difference nλ and minima at (n + ½)λ. Measure spacing between adjacent maxima or adjacent minima, not between a maximum and a minimum. Across N equal-type intervals use w = total span/N. Near the central region, with small angles and D sufficiently greater than source separation, w ≈ λD/d, so λ ≈ wd/D. This is an approximation, not a universal room-acoustics formula. Compare fλ with a reasonable room-temperature sound speed of approximately 340 m/s as a plausibility check, not as a measured value.

If the geometry or reflections prevent reliable fringe spacing, use a first off-centre maximum and measure distances r₁ and r₂ from both speaker centres: λ ≈ |r₁ − r₂| for the correctly identified first order; for a known order n, λ = |r₁ − r₂|/n. If order cannot be established, retain qualitative observations and identify why no valid wavelength can be claimed. Do not fabricate clean measurements.

Teaching and learning sequence

Teacher activity

Student activity

0–5 min: introduce the two-speaker setup and check prior ideas about superposition. Set a comfortable level and explain the measurement route.

Identify the two sources and recall how overlapping waves combine. Note the frequency and source arrangement.

5–12 min: run the sources at one fixed frequency and help students locate repeatable louder and quieter positions.

Move along the marked route in turns. Identify and mark repeated maxima or minima, keeping listening height consistent.

12–22 min: guide measurement of d, D and several equal-type fringe intervals. Check repeats and whether the geometry supports the approximation.

Record the geometry, interval count and span with units. Repeat a measurement and calculate mean spacing. Use the path-difference alternative if appropriate.

22–32 min: support wavelength calculations using the valid model and ask students to check plausibility and explain limitations.

Calculate wavelength from the measured pattern where valid, compare fλ with a reasonable sound speed, and identify the effects of reflections or uncertain positions.

32–40 min: plenary: ask for one explanation of a maximum/minimum, one evidence-supported result and one limitation. Collect evidence and record the next step.

Give an individual brief explanation and state a justified result or explain why a numerical wavelength was not reliable. Submit the measurement record.

Assessment, evidence and model-goal connection

Results headings: class/date; f (Hz); d (m); D (m); fringe type; start/end positions (m); number of intervals; span (m); w (m); λ (m); repeated result; limitation. Retain anonymised calculations and individual explanations, an apparatus photo, and a note of Haaris’s construction contribution. A functioning model or an audible pattern alone is not evidence that every learner understands interference.

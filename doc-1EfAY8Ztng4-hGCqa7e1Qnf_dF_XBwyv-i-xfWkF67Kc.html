Lesson 12: Circuit design challenge and assessment

Inquiry question Can we design a circuit that meets a specified need?

Classes

Y10 Science 1

Date

20 November 2026 - planned

Suggested duration

50–60 minutes

Lesson

12 of 12

Unit

Electricity and circuits

Teacher

Haaris

Whakataukī

He moana pukepuke e ekengia e te waka.

Meaning: A choppy sea can be navigated.

Why this lesson: Encourages perseverance and revision when troubleshooting the final circuit design challenge.

Curriculum alignment

Knowledge

  • Electric circuits provide a continuous path that allows charge to move through components.
  • Series circuits have a single continuous path for current, where total resistance increases with additional, and a single break stops all current.
  • Parallel circuits have multiple paths for current, where voltage is the same across branches and branch currents combine to form total current. A break in one path does not stop current in others.
  • Energy is transferred between components in a circuit as electric charge moves and interacts with those components.

Practices

  • Construct and test series and parallel circuits to observe current flow and circuit function.
  • Predict and test how adding or removing components affects current in series and parallel circuits.
  • Use appropriate scientific conventions when reporting about electricity.

Learning intention

We are learning to apply our understanding of circuits to design, test and explain a working solution.

Success criteria

  • I can produce a circuit diagram that meets the design criteria.
  • I can construct and safely test the circuit.
  • I can use evidence to explain and improve the design.
  • I can communicate using correct symbols, quantities and units.

Prior knowledge and likely misconceptions

Students have studied complete circuits, conductors, current, voltage, resistance, series and parallel arrangements, and energy transfer.

Teacher and student roles

Teacher Clarify criteria without supplying a solution. Approve plans before construction, monitor safe testing, use questions to prompt diagnosis and gather evidence of independent understanding.

Students Interpret the brief, plan, predict, construct, test, record evidence, modify and explain the final design.

Resources

  • Cells or low-voltage supplies
  • Lamps, buzzers, motors, resistors and switches as available
  • Connecting leads
  • Ammeters or voltmeters where appropriate
  • Design brief and assessment rubric

Safety

Use low-voltage components. The teacher must check circuits before power is applied. Avoid direct short circuits and disconnect components that become warm.

Teaching and learning sequence

Time and phase

Teacher actions

Student actions

Assessment and responsive support

8 min

Design brief

Introduce the practical need, design criteria and constraints.

Identify a suitable series, parallel or combined arrangement.

Check interpretation of the brief.

12 min

Plan and predict

Prompt a labelled plan and predictions about operation, breaks and changes.

Draw the circuit and predict its behaviour.

Check diagrams and safe feasibility before building.

20 min

Build and test

Supervise construction and testing against criteria, prompting systematic troubleshooting.

Build, test, collect evidence and troubleshoot.

Observe safe practice and distinguish conceptual reasoning from construction alone.

10 min

Improve and explain

Ask for one justified modification and evidence of its effect.

Improve the circuit and prepare an explanation.

Check that the modification addresses a criterion or observed problem.

10 min

Individual assessment / Plenary

Individual assessment and plenary (10 min): allow 6 minutes for the existing diagram, evidence statement and improvement reflection, then 4 minutes to revisit the unit's success criteria and summarise transferable ideas.

Submit an individual diagram and evidence-based reflection; identify one concept they can now apply and one remaining question.

Assess accuracy, function, evidence-based improvement and communication. Use remaining questions and individual work to decide the next teaching steps.

Assessment for learning

Use the design brief to assess scientific accuracy, circuit function, safe practice, evidence-based improvement and communication conventions.

Support and extension

Provide tiered briefs and a restricted component set for support. Extend students by requiring independent branches, an additional constraint or current/voltage evidence.

Questions to guide discussion

  • Which arrangement best meets the criteria?
  • What evidence shows that the circuit works as intended?
  • What caused the fault?
  • How did the modification improve the design?

Evidence to retain

Initial and revised circuit diagrams, teacher observation, test results, final explanation and individual reflection.

Post lesson reflection

  • Did the task reveal conceptual understanding rather than construction skill alone?
  • Which criteria students found difficult to satisfy?
  • How independently did students troubleshoot?
  • What does the evidence suggest should follow this unit?

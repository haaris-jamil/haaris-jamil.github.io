Week 6 Reflection

2–6 November 2026 | Placement Week 6

This reflection follows Rolfe et al.’s (2001) “What? So what? Now what?” framework.

What?

The contrast between this week’s practical activities challenged my assumptions about engagement. In Science 2, a twenty-minute recap preceded a voltage–current investigation that then encountered equipment problems. Outlet wiring using a 12 V supply engaged both groups more successfully. Most groups completed Science 1’s two-lamp challenge, although we did not finish the sequence. Construction sometimes displaced the exit check.

During Thursday’s lesson, two students remained distracted despite reminders. My mentor teacher spoke with them outside and changed their seating; the lesson then proceeded smoothly.

I also observed my mentor teacher challenge Year 13 students to play “Happy Birthday” on wine glasses. They adjusted water levels, listened and worked out the tune independently. He then connected their discoveries with standing waves. I was impressed by how his activity combined physics with students’ musical talents.

So what?

Both circuit activities were hands-on, so practical work alone cannot explain the different responses. The recognisable purpose of outlet wiring seemed important. My mentor teacher’s knowledge of the students helped me choose an activity that interested them.

The wine-glass lesson showed how a purposeful challenge can draw on learners’ strengths while teacher guidance connects experience with scientific explanation. I see a connection with Dewey’s account of inquiry and learners’ interests (Hildebrand, 2021, section 5.1). The task was my mentor teacher’s, but it influenced my planning.

Engagement still needs to be distinguished from understanding. A working circuit does not show whether every student can explain it, and missing the exit check limited my evidence. The seating intervention also reminded me to reconsider the learning environment when reminders are ineffective.

Now what?

I will retain meaningful contexts and use photographs to support the next two-lamp challenge. I will check whether students become more independent, rather than assume the support works. I will test equipment, prepare an alternative and ask brief individual questions during practical work. I also want to use challenges that draw on students’ interests, then deliberately connect their observations with the scientific explanation.

References

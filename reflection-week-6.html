Week 6 Reflection

Week 6 Reflection

2–6 November 2026 | Placement Week 6

This reflection follows Rolfe et al.’s (2001) “What? So what? Now what?” framework.

What?

This week, the contrast between the circuit-measurement investigation and the outlet-wiring activities helped me think more carefully about what makes a lesson meaningful for these students. In the circuit investigation, several students had missed the previous lesson, so I used the crossword to revisit the ideas they needed. This took about twenty minutes. The practical then ran into equipment problems, possibly faulty connecting leads, and I planned to revisit the measurements through a demonstration.

The outlet-wiring activities were much more successful in engaging both Year 10 groups. Students who do not always participate were involved, and the groups completed the task. In one lesson, the construction and testing took the time I had allocated to the exit check. In the two-lamp challenge, most groups completed the practical, although we did not finish the entire planned sequence. I had deliberately included additional work in case students finished early, so this was not unexpected.

There were also difficulties. In one lesson, two students remained distracted despite my reminders. My mentor teacher spoke with them outside and changed their seating, after which the lesson proceeded more smoothly.

So what?

My main learning this week is that knowing the subject and knowing the learners are different things, and both matter. My mentor teacher’s suggestions were pivotal because he has an established relationship with the students and understands their interests. I might not have chosen these particular activities for another class, but they worked well for these learners. Drawing on his knowledge helped me make better decisions while I continue developing my own understanding of them.

It would be too simple to conclude that students like practical work and dislike theory. Both the meter investigation and the outlet wiring involved building circuits, but the response was different. The connection to something recognisable outside the classroom seemed to give the outlet activity a clearer purpose. This challenges me to make the scientific ideas meaningful through the activity, rather than assume that doing something hands-on will be enough.

At the same time, engagement is not the same as understanding. Completing a working circuit does not necessarily mean that a student can explain it or represent it accurately. The worksheets give me a way to look beyond participation and identify what needs revisiting. Although I do not regret spending time on construction and testing, missing the exit check reduced the information I had about individual understanding.

The behaviour incident also showed me the limits of repeated verbal reminders. I want to maintain respectful relationships, but I need to recognise when a different intervention is required. My mentor teacher’s response gave me a useful example of acting clearly without allowing the disruption to dominate the lesson.

Now what?

I will continue choosing contexts that connect with these students’ lives, while keeping the scientific purpose explicit. For the next group completing the two-lamp challenge, I have added photographs of the completed circuit and a close-up of the switch terminals to help students get started. I will check whether these supports improve independence rather than assume that they will.

I will also use a brief individual question or explanation during practical work so that checking understanding does not depend entirely on having time at the end. Before the next investigation, I will test the apparatus and prepare a workable alternative. I will discuss with my mentor teacher how to follow up when reminders are ineffective. As we move towards electricity generation and the proposed field trip, I want to retain the practical relevance that worked this week while helping students explain what they observe.

References

Rolfe, G., Freshwater, D., & Jasper, M. (2001). Critical reflection for nursing and the helping professions: A user’s guide. Palgrave.

Y10 Electricity Unit Overview

This six-week, 12-lesson unit develops electric charge, circuits and energy transfer through practical work and evidence-based explanation. Revision 13 October 2026: Lesson 1 is the shared introduction; Lesson 2 develops the original static-charge outcomes because many learners did not know electrons are negative. Lesson 3 combines complete-circuit construction with a limited materials investigation, preserving space for later learning. The walking-distance beach generator inquiry replaces Te Uku. Y10 Science 2 has Lesson 2 on Thursday 15 October (my mentor teacher’s formal observation); Y10 Science 1 on Friday 16 October. The same broad sequence is adapted to each group’s evidence.

Classes

Y10 Science 1 and Y10 Science 2

Length

6 weeks

Lessons

12 per class

Frequency

2 lessons per week

Suggested duration

50–60 minutes

Total learning events

24 planned class deliveries; record actual teaching contributions separately

Unit question

How do electrical circuits transfer energy, and how can we design them to perform useful tasks?

Unit outcomes

  • Explain charge and static electricity, then current, voltage and resistance using models and experimental evidence.
  • Construct and compare series and parallel circuits safely.
  • Use ammeters and voltmeters appropriately.
  • Investigate conductors, resistance and changes to circuit behaviour.
  • Trace energy transfers and explain inefficiency without suggesting that energy disappears.
  • Evaluate electricity generation choices in an Aotearoa New Zealand context.
  • Apply circuit understanding in a design challenge.

Assessment approach

Use formative assessment throughout. Compare initial and exit charge responses in Lesson 2. Before circuits, check electron/proton signs, neutral balance, gain/loss of electrons and like/unlike-charge predictions. Reteach unresolved basics at the start of Lesson 3. Before meters in Lesson 4, check complete-path construction and safe circuit operation. Combine the Lesson 3 materials test with circuit construction; reduce/defer wider conductivity testing if needed. Later evidence includes diagrams, measurements, explanations and the beach investigation. Lesson 12 remains the integrated design assessment. Record adaptations separately for each class; do not assume a demonstration established understanding.

Equipment and preparation

Confirm cells/supplies, lamps, switches, leads, resistors, meters, resistance wire, charge counters, static materials, approved Van de Graaff equipment and the secured ramp/ping-pong balls. For Lesson 11, choose and bench-test one wind- or water-driven DC-motor generator with my mentor teacher. Confirm rotor/wheel coupling, measurable output, meter range, protection from water/sand and the investigation sheet. Agree the beach, walking route, date, EOTC approval, supervision, consent, access needs, weather/tide criteria and a school-based fallback. Walking time is additional to the 60-minute learning sequence and must fit an agreed visit window.

Safety and inclusion

Use approved low-voltage equipment, avoid short circuits and check meters. The Van de Graaff stays teacher-operated with school-approved screening, separation and discharge procedures. Secure the ramp and collect loose balls. The beach inquiry needs EOTC approval, an agreed route/site, supervision, weather/tide checks and accessible alternatives. Work away from surf; no water entry is part of the activity. Secure rotors, protect hands, keep motors/meters dry and use a contained water model rather than damming natural waterways or altering habitat. Collect all materials. Explain model limitations and use learner responses to set the pace.

Science Club Activity Plan – Design and Build Model Boats

ACTIVITY OVERVIEW

Group: Primary Science Club

Delivered:

Role: Planned and co-led

Suggested duration: 45–60 minutes

Inquiry question: How do a boat’s shape, construction and distribution of materials affect whether it floats and remains stable?

LEARNING INTENTION

Students investigate how design choices affect floating and stability by planning, building, testing and improving a model boat.

SUCCESS CRITERIA

Students collaboratively construct a boat that floats; explain at least one feature that improves floating or stability; and use observations from testing to justify an improvement.

RESOURCES

Playdough, aluminium foil, popsicle sticks, water troughs or large basins, trays, towels, paper and pencils. Optional: small identical masses for a fair load test.

PREPARATION

Set up water-testing stations on stable surfaces away from electrical equipment and walkways. Divide materials into equal group packs. Decide whether material choice is part of the challenge and prepare a space for drying and displaying boats.

TEACHING AND LEARNING SEQUENCE

Time and phase

Teacher actions

Student actions

Assessment and responsive support

5 min

Engage and predict

Show playdough and foil; elicit predictions about floating and changing shape.

Predict whether each floats and whether changing its shape could alter the result.

Record initial ideas for comparison with tests.

5 min

Introduce challenge

Explain materials, time, stability criteria and any load-test rule.

Restate the task and agree roles.

Check that each group understands the criteria.

15 min

Plan and build

Prompt consideration of width, symmetry, material distribution and load position.

Sketch, explain features and build the first model collaboratively.

Observe participation and check the reasoning behind design choices.

15–20 min

Test and improve

Supervise water stations and prompt one deliberate modification. If load testing, use identical masses.

Test, record observations, modify and retest. Record maximum supported load where used.

Check fair comparisons and whether evidence supports the improvement.

5–10 min

Plenary and share

Revisit the learning intention; invite each group to explain a successful feature, difficulty and evidence-based improvement. Ask each learner to name one design change and its observed effect.

Display the model, explain the improvement and contribute a brief individual response.

Use explanations to distinguish design reasoning from successful floating alone. Record what to retain or revisit; keep existing post-activity evidence separate from this revised future-use sequence.

GUIDING QUESTIONS

What changed when the same material was given a different shape? Where should material or load be placed to keep the boat stable? How can the test be kept fair? What evidence supports the proposed improvement?

ASSESSMENT AND EVIDENCE

Evidence includes completed boats, labelled designs, student explanations, photographs where permitted, load-test results where used, and teacher observations of collaboration and evidence-based improvement.

SUPPORT AND EXTENSION

Provide a simple hull outline or defined team roles where additional structure is needed. Extend the activity through a fair load test, comparison of hull shapes, control of the amount of material, or calculation of a simple load-to-material ratio.

SAFETY

Place water stations away from electrical equipment and walkways. Wipe spills immediately, keep floors clear, supervise small materials and require handwashing after handling shared playdough and water.

POST-ACTIVITY EVALUATION

Completed boats and observations during the activity provide the primary evidence. Add a brief note identifying which designs were most stable, which explanations demonstrated scientific thinking, and what should change if the activity is repeated.

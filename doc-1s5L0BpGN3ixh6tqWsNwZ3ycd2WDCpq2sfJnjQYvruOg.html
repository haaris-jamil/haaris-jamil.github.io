Lesson 06: Graphing demonstration and switched-outlet practical

Year 10 Science 2 · 5 Nov 2026 · Planned

Inquiry question: How can we make reliable connections and use a switch to control a lamp?

The previous lesson did not produce usable circuit readings. A brief teacher demonstration will complete the voltage-current investigation and introduce Ohm’s law before students apply complete-path reasoning to a switched-outlet practical. An arcade clip and my experience working in a gaming arcade will establish the practical context. This class-specific adaptation leaves the original unit plan unchanged.

Why this lesson: Students put their ideas into practice by making, checking and correcting connections to produce a working circuit.

Curriculum alignment

Knowledge

  • Resistance is a property of a material or component in a circuit that limits the movement of electric charge, measured in ohms (Ω).
  • Resistors are circuit components that have increased resistance.
  • Electric circuits provide a continuous path that allows charge to move through components.
  • Conductors (e.g. metals) allow the movement of electric charge.
  • Insulators (e.g. plastic) restrict the movement of electric charge.

Practices

  • Measure current and voltage in different parts of a circuit using appropriate instruments.
  • Construct and test series and parallel circuits to observe current flow and circuit function.
  • Use appropriate scientific conventions when reporting about electricity.

Learning intention

We are learning to interpret a demonstrated voltage-current relationship, make reliable connections to a switched outlet and explain how its switch controls a lamp.

Success criteria

  • I can describe the pattern in the demonstrated graph and use two readings to compare V ÷ I.
  • I can identify A, N and E on the outlet and explain their household functions.
  • I can make and check connections, test the lamp in both switch positions and explain which path the switch interrupts.

Resources

  • A checked adjustable low-voltage DC supply, fixed resistor of suitable power rating, ammeter, voltmeter and tested leads for the brief teacher demonstration. Safety

Use a current-limited low-voltage DC supply and a suitably rated fixed resistor for the opening demonstration. Check meter modes, sockets and ranges, and switch off before rewiring. For the outlet practical, students will construct, inspect and modify assemblies with the supply disconnected. I will inspect each assembly before controlling the protected, safety-isolated 12 V AC functional test. Conducting parts will be enclosed or protected during powered testing. Disconnect before students handle or adjust the assembly. Check protective-earth continuity separately while disconnected. The 12 V AC output has two secondary terminals, distinct from household active and neutral. Retain A, N and E as the fitting’s household labels. Label assemblies “12 V AC teaching model only”, provide no mains supply plug, and retain them at school. Supervise tools and collect offcuts.

Teaching and learning sequence

Assessment and responsive support

0–15 min

Arcade introduction and brief investigation demonstration

0–5: play the arcade clip as students arrive and settle. Stop it for a short account of my arcade work and connect reliable electrical connections to our previous circuit difficulties. Introduce the whakataukī and learning focus briefly. 5–15: demonstrate five paired voltage-current readings with the same fixed resistor, then quickly plot current horizontally and voltage vertically. Ask students to identify the pattern and compare two non-zero V ÷ I values before naming resistance and introducing V = IR at approximately constant temperature.

Watch the clip and listen to the brief personal introduction. During the demonstration, read selected meter values and record the displayed pairs where useful. Watch the graph being plotted, describe its pattern and compare two non-zero V ÷ I ratios with support. The graph is a teacher demonstration.

Use targeted questions and allow thinking time. Read displays aloud as well as showing them. Check the pattern and one ratio through student responses. This phase assesses interpretation of a demonstrated graph, rather than independent graph construction. Keep the phase within ten minutes so practical time is protected.

15–23 min

Household roles and task demonstration

Ask students for two possible causes of the failed circuits and one check. Explain household A (active/live), N (neutral) and E (protective earth), using the fitting and diagram. Trace the factory-wired switched active path and continuous neutral/earth paths. Model cable preparation and one secure connection. Compare the enclosed switch with the familiar knife switch. Distinguish household labels from the two 12 V AC supply outputs. Use slide 8 to compare scrap-metal values and connect a broken wire to an interrupted circuit. Explain that thicker copper conductors use more material: for the same length, doubling cross-sectional area doubles copper use. Discuss cost, weight and flexibility alongside reliability, emphasising suitable wire size and secure connections.

Complete the opening worksheet prompts, identify A, N and E on the fitting and follow the paths in the diagram. Observe the connection example, identify one quality check and agree paired practical roles. Compare the listed metal prices and explain one trade-off involved in using thicker copper wire.

Use the opening responses and first-connection explanations to identify who needs vocabulary support, an annotated example or a staged start. Check that learners distinguish neutral from protective earth.

23–48 min

Paired construction, inspection and testing

23–38: circulate during paired construction, checking each learner’s first preparation or connection. Provide an annotated example and staged prompts where needed. 38–48: inspect each assembly, supervise disconnected continuity checks and control the 12 V AC tests. Ask each student to trace a path or explain a check. Disconnect before corrections. Offer early finishers a disconnected fault diagnosis or path annotation.

Connect the supply cable to the factory-wired outlet while disconnected, using the checked example and worksheet diagram. Both partners take turns making and checking connections. Present the assembly for inspection, observe the supervised lamp test in both switch positions and record results individually. Make corrections only after disconnection.

Check both partners’ participation and explanations. Reduce support as learners become more independent. A working lamp provides a functional result, while individual questions, checks and worksheet entries provide evidence of each learner’s understanding.

48–60 min

Individual plenary and pack-up

48–55: revisit the learning intention and collect brief individual responses through the worksheet and questions. Ask which conductor the household outlet switch interrupts, one connection-quality check and why switching only neutral would leave the load connected to active. Include one brief question about the demonstrated voltage-current pattern. 55–60: collect work, disconnect the supply and supervise safe pack-up. Record each group’s stopping point.

Complete the brief worksheet exit responses or explain orally where appropriate. State one finding from the demonstrated graph. Submit work and pack away assemblies, tools and offcuts when instructed.

Prioritise a brief individual check before pack-up. If testing takes longer, use short oral checks during each group’s test and collect the worksheet at the end. Carry unfinished construction or testing forward, preserving inspection, disconnection and pack-up time.

Assessment for learning

Assessment will draw on responses to the demonstrated graph, each learner’s contribution to construction, connection inspections, disconnected continuity results, lamp test records and individual plenary responses. The demonstration does not provide evidence of students’ independent graph construction. A working lamp alone does not establish every learner’s understanding.

Support and extension

Differentiation will retain the same core outlet task and success criteria. Learners needing support will use an annotated terminal example, vocabulary prompts, practice cable and a first-connection check. I will model a step at the point of difficulty and ask students to trace one path at a time. Ready learners will work from the basic diagram and diagnose a disconnected fault after their core circuit is checked. Partners will alternate practical roles. Individual oral or written responses will distinguish each learner’s understanding. Keep the graphing demonstration brief and carry unfinished practical work forward if needed.

Questions to guide discussion

  • What does the demonstrated graph tell us about voltage and current?
  • Which path does the outlet switch interrupt, and how can we check it?
  • What connection-quality check did you make, and what problem could it reveal?

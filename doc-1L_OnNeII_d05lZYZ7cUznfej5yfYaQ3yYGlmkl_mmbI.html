Lesson 03: Energy supply and two-switch circuit challenges

Year 10 Science 2 · 22 Oct 2026 · Completed

Inquiry question What does the cell supply, and what controls a lamp in a one-loop circuit?

Whakataukī

Whāia te mātauranga hei oranga mō koutou.

Meaning: Seek knowledge for the wellbeing of all.

Why this lesson: Learners use a controlled test and counterexample to evaluate circuit claims, then apply the same reasoning to interpreting materials-test evidence.

Curriculum alignment

Knowledge

  • Electric circuits provide a continuous path that allows charge to move through components.
  • Conductors (e.g. metals) allow the movement of electric charge.
  • Insulators (e.g. plastic) restrict the movement of electric charge.
  • Energy is transferred between components in a circuit as electric charge moves and interacts with those components.

Practices

  • Construct and test series and parallel circuits to observe current flow and circuit function.
  • Predict and test how adding or removing components affects current in series and parallel circuits.
  • Test and classify materials as conductors or insulators using simple circuits.
  • Use appropriate scientific conventions when reporting about electricity.

Learning intention

We are learning to explain the cell's energy-supplying role and investigate, represent and explain a complete circuit.

Success criteria

  • I can use the ramp model to explain qualitatively what the cell supplies to charge.
  • I can test two switch positions and all four two-switch combinations, then justify a rule from evidence.

I can draw my one-cell, one-lamp, two-switch circuit in my exercise book using standard symbols.

Resources

  • One low-voltage cell/holder, one lamp/holder, TWO switches and sufficient leads per pair; pre-tested spare kit. Label switches S1 and S2. No second lamp is needed. Secure ramp, identical balls and a return/lifting route for the energy model.
  • Two contrasting samples such as metal and plastic for an optional extension; dry wood or graphite only if extra time remains.

Unfinished Lesson 2 worksheet for the Do now; exercise books and pens; circuit-symbol reference. The final five-question exit ticket is displayed on screen and answered with one word per item in exercise books; no printed ticket. Materials samples: clean paperclip, plastic and foil.

Safety

Secure the ramp and keep balls contained. Use low-voltage cells only. Disconnect the cell before moving switches/cell or changing connections. Keep one conducting loop, with both switches inserted into that loop. Never bridge the lamp, short the cell, or introduce branches.

Assessment and responsive support

0–5 min

Do now and recap check

0–3: direct completion of priority Lesson 2 items. 3–5: elicit the key charge explanation and briefly connect the whakataukī and learning focus.

Finish priority items and explain the charge concept.

Identify any charge misconception before circuits; retain other unfinished items for follow-up.

5–36 min

Ramp model, circuit demonstration and challenges

5–10: demonstrate ramp energy supply and limitations. 10–15: demonstrate the safe one-lamp circuit. 15–19: check starting loops. 19–26: set switch-location challenges. 26–32: set the two-switch, four-combination challenge in the same loop. 32–36: require circuit drawings.

Connect ramp energy transfers to the cell and lamp. Build, predict, test and swap roles. Derive a switch rule from evidence; draw and label the tested circuit individually.

Check complete-path reasoning and symbols. Elicit explanations before consolidating outcomes.

36–51 min

Predict–test–explain materials investigation

36–39: establish a working tester and teach conductor/insulator. 39–46: supervise sample tests and verification with a known metal paperclip. 46–51: discuss connection faults and no-light results.

Predict plastic and foil results, test samples while changing only the sample, swap roles, verify contacts and explain results orally.

Distinguish an insulator from a faulty connection. If building is slow, use a shared tester while retaining individual prediction and explanation.

51–60 min

Consolidation, quick check and pack-up / Plenary

Plenary (51–56): revisit the learning intention and display the existing five short exit questions. Pack-up (56–60): direct disconnection and equipment return.

Write numbers 1–5 with one-word answers in exercise books, then return equipment safely.

Inspect individual responses and circuit drawings; note complete-path or conductor misconceptions to revisit.

Hear each learner's ramp-model explanation, switch prediction and evidence-based rule. Check their two-switch circuit diagram in exercise books. Display-only exit ticket (5 minutes): 1 What does the cell supply to charge? 2 Which term describes the cell's energy supply? 3 Lamp on: both switches must be ____. 4 Lamp on: the conducting path must be ____. 5 Moving the cell along this loop changes the rule: yes/no? Teacher-only key: energy; EMF; closed; complete (accept unbroken/closed); no. No answers appear on the learner slide.

Check charge explanations, each learner's switch-position prediction, the controlled comparison and the explanation using the whole path. Assess whether students can construct and defend a counterexample, then distinguish a material result from a connection fault. Finish with the existing four-item Quick check; no additional worksheet.

Support: a labelled kit, one verified working starting loop, role swaps, oral predictions and a circuit-symbol reference. Each partner must explain and draw; do not let one do all construction. Extension: evaluate whether a closed switch guarantees a lit lamp and propose another possible break without being shown the solution. Do not demonstrate the two-switch result before students test. The ramp is an energy analogy: gravity drives balls, an electric field drives charge; the cell's chemical processes transfer energy. Balls can roll down an isolated ramp, unlike sustained current which requires a complete path. Height difference maps to energy supplied per unit charge qualitatively; no equations or units are assessed.

Continue from Lesson 2 with worksheet completion as the Do now. Use a labelled kit or a working reference circuit for support. Extend through evaluating the claim that electrons still 'on their way' should keep the lamp glowing after opening the switch; focus on the complete-path requirement rather than quantitative transients. If construction is slow, use a shared tester for materials but preserve the switch and claim reasoning.

Questions to guide discussion

  • What in the ramp model represents the cell supplying energy, and what represents energy being transferred at the lamp?
  • What rule fits all four combinations of S1 and S2? Does changing their locations or the cell's location change your evidence?
  • How can your circuit diagram and tracing of the complete path support your rule?

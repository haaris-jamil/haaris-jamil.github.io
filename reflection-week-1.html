Week 1 Reflection

This reflection follows Rolfe et al.’s (2001) “What? So what? Now what?” framework.

What?

During my first week at my placement school, I observed several Year 10 science lessons taught by my mentor teacher. Other teachers had described this cohort as challenging. In my mentor teacher’s lessons, however, the students generally remained on task and continued working even when the activities were difficult. A visiting student teacher who had seen some of these students in other classes was also surprised by how focused they were.

So what?

Initially, I thought this was largely because of my mentor teacher’s calm but authoritative presence, his humour and the relationships he had built with the students. However, as I observed more closely, I realised that there was more to it. He arranged the tables as islands for group work, in rows for individual tasks and in a circle for discussions. He also used a variety of activities and physical models to explain ideas. This helped me appreciate how his planning and classroom organisation supported the students’ engagement, alongside his personality and rapport with them.

At the same time, I understand that being on task does not necessarily mean that a student has understood the science. Fredricks et al. (2004) distinguish between behavioural, emotional and cognitive engagement. The students’ persistence and apparent enjoyment suggested the first two, but I would need to hear their explanations to understand more about their thinking. This is something I want to pay closer attention to in my observations.

Now what?

I had intended to speak with students during breaks, but other commitments made this difficult. I have instead begun initiating brief individual conversations during lessons. In future observations, I will pay closer attention to students’ explanations, predictions and misconceptions. This should allow me to judge engagement through evidence of their thinking, rather than merely through visible compliance.

References

Fredricks, J. A., Blumenfeld, P. C., & Paris, A. H. (2004). School engagement: Potential of the concept, state of the evidence. Review of Educational Research, 74(1), 59–109. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543074001059

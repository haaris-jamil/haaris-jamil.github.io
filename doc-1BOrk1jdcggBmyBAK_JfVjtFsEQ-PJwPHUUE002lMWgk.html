Practical household wiring: a 12 V AC model

Full lesson plan | Year 10 Science | Teacher: Haaris Jamil | Duration: 60 minutes

Class: __________ Date / period: __________ Room: __________

Lesson purpose and prior learning

This lesson builds on the difficulties students encountered when constructing circuits in the previous practical. I will use those experiences to explore why reliable connections matter, particularly in household wiring. Students will connect and test a factory-wired switched outlet using an isolated 12 V AC supply, then construct a separate parallel circuit in which two lamps can be controlled independently. The emphasis is on practical participation, careful checking and explaining the behaviour of a working circuit.

Whakataukī

Mā te kimi ka kite, mā te kite ka mōhio, mā te mōhio ka mārama.

Meaning: Through seeking comes discovery; through discovery comes knowledge; through knowledge comes understanding.

Justification: Students move from investigating a failed circuit to making, checking and explaining a working connection.

Curriculum alignment

Knowledge

  • Electric circuits provide a continuous path that allows charge to move through components.
  • Conductors (e.g. metals) allow the movement of electric charge.
  • Insulators (e.g. plastic) restrict the movement of electric charge.
  • Parallel circuits have multiple paths for current, where voltage is the same across branches and branch currents combine to form total current. A break in one path does not stop current in others.

Practices

  • Construct and test series and parallel circuits to observe current flow and circuit function.
  • Use appropriate scientific conventions when reporting about electricity.

Learning intention

We are learning how household conductors and fittings function, and how to make and check reliable connections in a 12 V AC model.

Success criteria

  • I can identify active/live, neutral and protective earth on the actual fitting and explain their household functions.
  • I can identify A, N and E, connect a supply cable to the outlet model and explain which internal path the switch interrupts.
  • I can inspect connections and use disconnected continuity results to identify a fault or justify a correction.
  • I can operate two parallel lamps independently, record all four switch combinations, and distinguish MCB and RCD protection.

Resources

Factory-wired switched socket outlets; separate two-gang changeover switch plates; compatible electrical cable; two 12 V AC-rated lamps and suitable holders per practical set; screwdrivers, wire strippers and cutters; continuity meters; protected connectors and suitable enclosures; the student worksheet and circuit diagrams.

A safety-isolated 12 V AC school power supply with current limiting or fusing appropriate to the lamps, cable and fittings is required for functional testing. The heating demonstration requires purpose-built enclosed apparatus with a visible temperature indication, operated within the manufacturer’s limits and the school’s approved procedure.

Before the lesson, I will test both practical circuits and verify the supply protection and lamp ratings. I will identify the outlet terminals marked A, N and E and check that its switch interrupts the active path while neutral and earth remain continuous. I will also identify the common and selected contacts on the separate changeover switches. The examples and diagrams will use the same fittings as the students’ practical work.

A completed switched-outlet example and two disconnected fault examples will support the introduction. I will arrange a teacher-operated testing station so that completed circuits can be checked as groups become ready. The heating demonstration will proceed when suitable apparatus and its approved operating procedure are available; otherwise, an illustration will support the explanation.

Safety and organisation

Students will prepare, connect, inspect and modify their assemblies with the supply disconnected. I will inspect each assembly before controlling its connection to the 12 V AC supply. Conducting parts will be enclosed or otherwise protected during powered testing. Each assembly will be disconnected before it is returned to students for handling or adjustment.

I will use the supply’s verified isolation and appropriately rated output protection, secure the fittings and supervise tool use. The heating apparatus will remain within its rated operating limits, with a clear observation boundary and sufficient cooling time. I will stop the demonstration if there is unexpected heating, odour or equipment behaviour. The demonstration will show a controlled temperature rise within the apparatus’s normal operating range.

Assemblies will be labelled “12 V AC teaching model only”, kept separate from equipment in mains service and retained or dismantled at school. Student assemblies will have no mains supply plug. Powered testing will take place once the supply, protected connections and compatible load have been checked; disconnected inspection and continuity testing provide the alternative where these requirements cannot be met.

The household functions of active, neutral and protective earth will be distinguished from the two isolated output terminals used in the 12 V AC model. Earth continuity will be checked separately. The model represents circuit connections and switching; household earthing and protective-device operation will be addressed through explanation and examples. AC will be introduced qualitatively as current that reverses direction.

Teaching and learning sequence

Teacher activity and timing

Student activity

0–6 min | Why did our circuits fail?

I will ask students to suggest possible reasons for the previous circuit failures and explain how they could investigate them. I will distinguish an interrupted path from a poor connection, then connect these ideas to reliability and safety in the home. A short circuit will be introduced as an unintended low-resistance path that can allow excessive current.

Students will record two possible causes, discuss a suitable check with a partner and share their reasoning. They will consider why an unreliable household connection has consequences beyond a device failing to operate.

6–12 min | Controlled heating

I will demonstrate a controlled temperature rise using the enclosed apparatus within its approved operating limits. I will draw attention to the temperature indication and explain how excessive current can overheat conductors. The discussion will distinguish the heating observed from the short-circuit conditions it helps us understand.

Students will observe the temperature change, record one finding and complete the short worksheet statement about resistance and current. They will relate the observation to the risk of overheating.

12–18 min | Conductors, switches and Activity 1

I will introduce A as active (live), N as neutral and E as protective earth, and trace the factory-built paths through the outlet. I will compare the familiar knife switch with the enclosed rocker and explain the purpose of the insulating casing. I will demonstrate cable preparation and connection using the labelled fixture, and briefly relate the whakataukī to investigating and checking our work.

Students will match conductor functions, identify A, N and E on the fixture and trace Diagram 1. They will identify the path interrupted by the switch and explain the purpose of the insulating casing.

18–33 min | Activity 1: connect and investigate a switched outlet

I will observe students’ first connections, inspect each disconnected assembly and supervise continuity checks of the switched active path and continuous neutral and earth paths. After checking the enclosure and connections, I will test the compatible lamp through the socket using the isolated 12 V AC supply.

Students will connect the supply cable to the outlet using the checked example and terminal markings, with both partners contributing. The factory-built internal connections will remain intact. Students will present their assembly for inspection and record the lamp’s state in both switch positions during the functional test.

33–52 min | Activity 2: separate switches and parallel lamps

I will introduce Diagram 2 as a separate circuit with two parallel branches, each containing a switch and lamp. I will support construction, check completed circuits and control the 12 V AC tests. Once students have recorded all four switch combinations, I will ask them to explain the results.

Students will construct Diagram 2 using the separate switches and lamps, with no socket outlets. Both partners will take part in making and checking connections. After inspection, they will record the four switch combinations and use the completed paths to explain the lamps’ behaviour.

52–56 min | Protection matching

I will briefly explain MCB and RCD functions using illustrations or disconnected examples. I will discuss students’ matching responses and relate these devices to the wider importance of reliable connections and insulation.

Students will match overcurrent protection to an MCB and current-imbalance protection to an RCD, then identify one connection-quality check.

56–60 min | Plenary and pack-up

I will revisit the learning intention and collect the individual exit responses. I will note the stage reached by each group, disconnect the supply and supervise the collection of assemblies, tools and offcuts.

Students will identify one connection-quality check and explain why opening one switch affects only its own parallel branch. They will submit the worksheet and pack away the equipment.

Assessment and differentiation

I will assess understanding through students’ proposed causes of circuit failure, their observations of heating, conductor identification, practical contributions and recorded lamp results. I will check each learner’s first preparation or connection and use the individual exit responses to identify ideas that need revisiting. Successful operation will be considered alongside inspection and continuity checks.

Students needing support will have access to an annotated example, conductor-function prompts and the inspection checklist. I will check their first connection and provide additional practice cable where needed. I will adapt tools or practical roles to support participation, while checking each student’s understanding through targeted questions and sufficient thinking time.

Students who finish early will diagnose a disconnected fault, trace the two parallel paths or explain why an open branch leaves the other lamp operating. Further disconnected continuity testing can extend their understanding of the changeover switch.

Inspection checklist

  • Connections match the checked diagram and actual terminal markings.
  • Conductors are securely retained using the component manufacturer’s method.
  • Insulation remains intact; exposed conductor is appropriately contained and stray strands are controlled.
  • Cable support, enclosure and covers are suitable for the test arrangement.
  • The assembly is disconnected and ready for inspection and continuity testing.

Scientific understanding

A short circuit can allow excessive current, while a loose or high-resistance connection can cause local heating without a short circuit. The controlled demonstration illustrates the heating effect of current. The cause of the previous circuit failures remains unconfirmed. Worksheet and circuit diagrams: View portfolio evidence

A denotes active (live), N denotes neutral and E denotes protective earth. In the single-pole outlet used for this model, the switch interrupts the active path while neutral and earth remain continuous. Interrupting only neutral would stop normal load current while leaving parts connected to active. Active and neutral carry the normal household load current; protective earth provides a fault path. Neutral remains potentially hazardous. Common identifies a changeover-switch contact and is distinct from neutral.

An MCB responds to excessive current, including overloads and short circuits. An RCD detects an imbalance between outgoing and returning current and provides additional protection against some electric-shock situations. An RCBO combines these functions. Illustrations or disconnected examples will support this explanation; the isolated 12 V circuit does not reproduce household RCD operation.

Plenary: three brief individual responses

  • Match excessive current to MCB protection and current imbalance to RCD protection.
  • Name one connection-quality check and the problem it helps identify.
  • Explain briefly why one lamp can remain on when the other switch is open.

Timing and carryover

The timings allow for both practical activities while retaining an individual plenary. I will check circuits as groups become ready and prioritise completion of the switched-outlet task. If construction or testing requires more time, the parallel-lamp activity will continue in the following lesson. I will record each group’s stopping point so that work can resume appropriately. The outstanding voltage/current graphing remains a separate catch-up task.

Evidence of learning

Evidence will include anonymised photographs of practical work, selected inspection records and corrected faults, continuity and functional-test observations, and individual exit responses. I will use these alongside my observations to evaluate learning and identify next steps. Delivery dates and reflections will be recorded separately for the two classes.

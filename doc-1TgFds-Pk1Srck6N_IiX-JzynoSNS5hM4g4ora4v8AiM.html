Lesson 8 Parallel circuits

Inquiry question Why are household circuits connected in parallel?

Classes

Y10 Science 1 and Y10 Science 2

Date

Suggested duration

50–60 minutes

Lesson

8 of 12

Unit

Electricity and circuits

Teacher

Haaris

Whakataukī

Nāu te rourou, nāku te rourou, ka ora ai te iwi.

Meaning: With your contribution and mine, the people will flourish.

Why this lesson: Connects contributions within practical groups with combining evidence about parallel branches.

Curriculum alignment

Knowledge

  • Parallel circuits have multiple paths for current, where voltage is the same across branches and branch currents combine to form total current. A break in one path does not stop current in others.

Practices

  • Construct and test series and parallel circuits to observe current flow and circuit function.
  • Measure current and voltage in different parts of a circuit using appropriate instruments.
  • Predict and test how adding or removing components affects current in series and parallel circuits.
  • Use appropriate scientific conventions when reporting about electricity.

Learning intention

We are learning to explain how current and voltage behave in a parallel circuit.

Success criteria

  • I can construct and represent a parallel circuit.
  • I can use measurements to compare branch and total current.
  • I can explain why one branch can operate independently of another.

Prior knowledge and likely misconceptions

Students understand complete circuits, current measurement, voltage measurement and the behaviour of a single-path series circuit.

Teacher and student roles

Teacher Check each circuit before power is applied. Ask students to predict before each change. Use measured values rather than brightness alone to establish current and voltage patterns.

Students Construct, test, measure, record, compare and explain a two-branch circuit. Revise initial predictions using evidence.

Resources

  • Low-voltage supplies or cells
  • Two lamps or resistors per group
  • Leads and switches
  • Ammeters and voltmeters or multimeters
  • Measurement table

Safety

Use low voltage. Check meter settings and connections. Never place an ammeter directly across a supply.

Teaching and learning sequence

Time and phase

Teacher actions

Student actions

Assessment and responsive support

8 min

Retrieval and contrast

Review series features and elicit predictions about a second path.

Recall series behaviour and predict a second-path effect.

Identify which series assumptions students may transfer to parallel circuits.

10 min

Teacher model

Build a two-branch circuit and trace junctions, branches and complete paths.

Trace each path and identify junctions and meter locations.

Check branch identification and safe meter positioning.

25 min

Practical investigation

Check circuits before power; supervise branch opening and current/voltage measurements.

Construct, test independent branches, measure branch and total currents, and compare branch voltages.

Use measured values to assess patterns; provide colour-coded diagrams or partially assembled junctions where needed.

10 min

Sense-making

Elicit patterns from the measurements and connect them to independently operated household appliances.

Explain current and voltage patterns and independent branch operation.

Probe whether explanations are grounded in measurements.

7 min

Exit check / Plenary

Plenary (7 min): revisit the success criteria and present the existing unfamiliar parallel diagram. Ask for total current from branch readings and an explanation of what happens if one branch opens.

Interpret the diagram individually, calculate or identify total current and explain branch independence.

Collect responses; check current splitting and common branch voltage. Use misconceptions to shape the next comparison lesson.

Assessment for learning

Construction accuracy, measurement table, explanations during questioning and exit response.

Support and extension

Provide a partially assembled junction or colour-coded branch diagram. Extend students by predicting total current for unequal branches and explaining why adding a branch affects the source current.

Questions to guide discussion

  • Where does current split and recombine?
  • What evidence shows that the branches operate independently?
  • How does total current compare with branch currents?
  • Why is the same voltage available across each branch?

Evidence to retain

Circuit diagram, completed measurement table, teacher observation of meter use, group explanation and exit response.

Post lesson reflection

  • Did students distinguish current splitting from voltage splitting?
  • Were meter readings reliable enough to support the intended pattern?
  • How did students connect independent branches to household use?
  • What adaptation should be made for the second class or later lesson?

Lesson 09: Turbine design challenge

Year 10 Science 2 · 16 Nov 2026 · Planned

Inquiry question Which circuit arrangement is more suitable for a particular purpose?

Classes

Y10 Science 2

Date

16 November 2026 - planned

Suggested duration

50–60 minutes

Lesson

9 of 12

Unit

Electricity and circuits

Teacher

Haaris

Whakataukī

Mā te huruhuru ka rere te manu.

Meaning: Adorn the bird with feathers so that it may fly.

Why this lesson: Highlights how knowledge and suitable tools enable informed choices between circuit designs.

Curriculum alignment

Knowledge

  • Series circuits have a single continuous path for current, where total resistance increases with additional, and a single break stops all current.
  • Parallel circuits have multiple paths for current, where voltage is the same across branches and branch currents combine to form total current. A break in one path does not stop current in others.

Practices

  • Construct and test series and parallel circuits to observe current flow and circuit function.
  • Measure current and voltage in different parts of a circuit using appropriate instruments.
  • Predict and test how adding or removing components affects current in series and parallel circuits.
  • Use appropriate scientific conventions when reporting about electricity.

Learning intention

We are learning to select and justify a circuit arrangement for a stated purpose.

Success criteria

  • I can compare series and parallel circuits accurately.
  • I can use observations or measurements as evidence.
  • I can justify a design choice for a particular application.

Resources

  • Series and parallel circuit equipment
  • Comparison table
  • Application scenario cards

Safety

Use low-voltage circuits and check connections before powering.

Prior learning and likely misconceptions

Students bring evidence from single-loop circuits and the independently switched lamps. I will check whether they distinguish a break in one branch from a break in a shared supply path, and whether they treat current as being used up. The comparison will follow their observations rather than precede them.

Preparation and organisation

I will pretest the available low-voltage equipment and prepare two matched circuit examples, a comparison grid and the two scenario briefs. I will confirm that the number of leads permits complete working stations and plan role rotation around the available sets. The scenarios are classroom models, not instructions for constructing household wiring or safety systems.

Teaching and learning sequence

Time and phase

Teacher actions

Student actions

Assessment and responsive support

8 min

Retrieval grid

I will display a comparison grid covering conducting paths, switching, the effect of a break and meter placement. Learners will first record what they remember, then compare answers with a partner. I will introduce the whakataukī and learning intention, and select uncertain claims for practical testing without supplying the outcomes.

Students will complete the retrieval grid individually, compare explanations with a partner and mark claims they want to test. They will refer to their independently switched lamps from Lesson 8 when identifying separate paths.

Identify uncertain claims to test.

20 min

Verification practical

I will demonstrate the agreed low-voltage setup and check each circuit before power is applied. Groups will compare one complete series circuit with one complete parallel circuit using the same supply and comparable lamps. I will ask them to test the effect of opening a switch or removing a lamp, changing one feature at a time. Where meters are available, I will check their connections before students record supporting readings.

Students will sketch both arrangements, rotate building, checking and recording roles, and record what happens to each lamp when a selected path is opened. They will disconnect the supply before altering connections and revise their original claims using the results. Each learner will retain an individual comparison record.

I will check that a claimed difference is supported by the test actually carried out. Learners needing support will trace each complete path on a labelled diagram before testing. Ready learners will distinguish a branch break from a break in a shared supply connection and justify the different consequences.

18 min

Design scenarios

I will present two specific briefs: model room lights that must operate independently, and a model warning circuit in which opening any one of several contacts must extinguish a warning lamp. I will ask pairs to identify the requirement before choosing a circuit arrangement. I will question their reasoning rather than provide a completed design.

Students will choose one brief, draw a labelled circuit and explain how its paths meet the stated requirement. They will use a result from the practical as evidence, then consider what the alternative arrangement would change.

I will provide the frame 'I recommend ... because our test showed ...' where needed and allow oral rehearsal before individual writing. I will ask ready learners to add a further switching requirement or identify what would happen if a shared connection failed.

14 min

Justification / Plenary

I will allow six minutes for an individual labelled diagram and evidence-based recommendation, then four minutes to compare selected responses against the success criteria. I will ask each learner which circuit feature was decisive and what evidence supports their choice. The final four minutes are reserved for disconnection, collection of apparatus and submission of work.

Students will complete their own recommendation and identify the decisive circuit feature and one limitation. They will submit their comparison and diagram, disconnect the supply and return equipment when directed.

Collect individual justifications and check whether the chosen arrangement meets the stated need. Identify concepts to revisit before the design challenge.

Assessment for learning

I will assess the initial and revised comparison, safe construction and checking, the evidence used to revise a claim, and each learner's final diagram and recommendation. A working group circuit will be considered alongside individual explanations. I will retain anonymised work and record misconceptions and support needed for the next lesson.

Support and extension

I will retain the same core comparison for all learners. Support will include labelled diagrams, a partly structured comparison grid, a first-connection check and oral rehearsal. I will reduce these prompts as understanding becomes secure. Extension will require distinguishing branch and shared-path faults or meeting an additional design constraint. If testing takes longer, I will carry the design scenario forward while retaining a brief individual evidence-based conclusion and safe pack-up.

Questions to guide discussion

  • Which feature matters most for this application?
  • What evidence supports your choice?
  • What trade-off does the alternative arrangement introduce?

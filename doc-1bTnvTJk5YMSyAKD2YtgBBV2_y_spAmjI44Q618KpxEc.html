Lesson 7 Series circuits

Inquiry question What happens when components are added in series?

Classes

Y10 Science 1 and Y10 Science 2

Date

Suggested duration

50–60 minutes

Lesson

7 of 12

Unit

Electricity and circuits

Teacher

Haaris

Whakataukī

Waiho i te toipoto, kaua i te toiroa.

Meaning: Let us keep close together, not far apart.

Why this lesson: Supports collaboration while students investigate the continuity of a series circuit.

Curriculum alignment

Knowledge

  • Series circuits have a single continuous path for current, where total resistance increases with additional, and a single break stops all current.

Practices

  • Construct and test series and parallel circuits to observe current flow and circuit function.
  • Predict and test how adding or removing components affects current in series and parallel circuits.
  • Use appropriate scientific conventions when reporting about electricity.

Learning intention

We are learning to predict and explain the behaviour of series circuits.

Success criteria

  • I can identify a series circuit from its diagram.
  • I can predict the effects of adding a component or creating a break.
  • I can explain observations using current and resistance.

Resources

  • Cells, lamps or resistors, switches and leads
  • Ammeters if available
  • Prediction-observation-explanation sheet

Safety

Use low voltage and avoid direct short circuits.

Teaching and learning sequence

Time and phase

Teacher actions

Student actions

Assessment and responsive support

7 min

Circuit identification

Display circuit diagrams and elicit single-path identification.

Trace paths and identify series circuits.

Check path tracing before prediction.

10 min

Predict

Present the addition of a second lamp or resistor and elicit predictions.

Predict brightness and current, with a reason.

Retain predictions for comparison with measurements.

23 min

Test

Supervise low-voltage construction, component changes and opening switches at different positions.

Construct, compare outcomes and record observations or current readings; test a break in the path.

Check safe connections and use measurements where available to support explanations.

15 min

Explain / Plenary

Explanation and plenary (15 min): use 10 minutes to discuss evidence through current, total resistance and a single path. Reserve 5 minutes for an individual response: what happens when a second resistive component is added at the same supply voltage, and what happens at a break?

Explain observations, then answer the two short closing prompts using the circuit path and evidence.

Check the link between added total resistance and current, and why a break affects the whole loop. Use responses to prepare the comparison with parallel circuits.

Assessment for learning

Predictions, observations, current data where available and explanations.

Support and extension

Provide physical circuit cards for students who find diagrams abstract. Extend students by comparing identical and different lamps.

Questions to guide discussion

  • How many routes can charge follow?
  • Why does one break affect every component?
  • What changes when total resistance increases?

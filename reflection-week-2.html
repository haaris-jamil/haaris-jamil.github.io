Week 2 Reflection

Reflection model: Rolfe et al.’s What? So what? Now what? model

During Week 2, my mentor’s involvement in the school production meant that I took greater responsibility for a Year 11 science class, supported by a relieving teacher. The students were expected to continue a ramp investigation and complete an accompanying worksheet. my mentor had asked me to respond to individual needs rather than enforce discipline. This left me with a familiar scientific task but an unfamiliar class whose relationships and routines I had not yet established.

Initially, only about one-third of the class appeared engaged. I approached individuals and asked whether they needed help rather than directing them to work. This brought several students back to the task, and engagement appeared to increase to roughly half the class. Progress in their written responses provided some evidence that learning continued. However, a substantial group remained disengaged despite repeated offers of assistance. I respected my mentor’s instruction not to assume a disciplinary role, but the experience exposed the limits of subject knowledge on its own. Knowing the physics concepts did not automatically give me the authority, relationships or contextual knowledge needed to teach the class effectively.

Afterwards, my mentor and I reflected on the lesson. He felt that the preceding whole-class discussion had not prepared students sufficiently for independent work. I also recognised that my ability to respond was limited because I knew little about the students, including what interested them and what forms of support they would accept. This is consistent with Roorda et al. (2011), who found that positive teacher-student relationships are associated with student engagement.

My next priority is therefore not to manufacture authority, but to build familiarity and trust through brief individual interactions, learning students’ names and interests, and observing how my mentor re-engages particular learners. Before taking responsibility again, I will also clarify the lesson structure, expected outcomes and boundaries of my role.

Reference

Roorda, D. L., Koomen, H. M. Y., Spilt, J. L., & Oort, F. J. (2011). The influence of affective teacher–student relationships on students’ school engagement and achievement: A meta-analytic approach. Review of Educational Research, 81(4), 493–529. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654311421793

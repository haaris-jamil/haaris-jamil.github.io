Week 2 Reflection

This reflection follows Rolfe et al.’s (2001) “What? So what? Now what?” framework.

What?

During Week 2, I took greater responsibility for a Year 11 science class while my mentor teacher was involved in the school production. A relieving teacher was present, and my role was to support individual learners rather than enforce discipline. Students were continuing a ramp investigation and worksheet.

Initially, about one-third appeared engaged. Offering help individually brought some back to the task, and participation increased to roughly half the class. Written responses showed some progress, but many remained disengaged despite repeated offers of assistance.

So what?

This experience helped me realise that knowing the subject is only one part of teaching. While I understood the physics, I knew little about the students, their relationships or the support they would respond to. My mentor teacher also felt that the discussion before the task had not prepared them sufficiently to work independently.

Roorda et al. (2011) link positive teacher–student relationships with engagement. This helped me understand the importance of knowing the students and giving them a clear starting point, in addition to explaining the content. I was encouraged that more students participated when I offered individual help, although I still needed to find out what they had understood.

Now what?

Before taking responsibility again, I will clarify the lesson structure, expected outcomes and boundaries of my role. I will continue learning students’ names and interests through brief conversations and observe how my mentor teacher re-engages particular learners. I also want to check whether students can explain their work, rather than judge success only by how many appear occupied.

References

Roorda, D. L., Koomen, H. M. Y., Spilt, J. L., & Oort, F. J. (2011). The influence of affective teacher–student relationships on students’ school engagement and achievement: A meta-analytic approach. Review of Educational Research, 81(4), 493–529. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654311421793
